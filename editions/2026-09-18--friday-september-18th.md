## Weather & Weekend Rundown:

Late newsletter, but well worth the wait! Spoiler: new tees out now!! But for later on, grab a jacket before you head out tonight. Saturday is the pick of the weekend, sunny, calm and cool with a high near 64. ['Our job is to make mistakes'; New Vermont research hub puts farming practices to the test](https://www.mynbc5.com/article/farming-research-hub-opens/73779965)

❝

*"Basically, our job is to make mistakes, so that farmers don't," per NBC5.*

The Food Systems Research Station at Nordic Farm officially opened Thursday after two years of prep by UVM and the USDA, which signed a 30 year lease on the site. One early project puts GPS collars on cows to track how they move and which forage they prefer, with research on grazing systems, crop production and adapting to changing conditions to follow. Officials said much of the country's grazing research has focused on the South, Midwest and West, so Vermont's heavy clay soils finally get their own test bed, and the next step is finishing the barn renovation so animals can arrive over the next few months.

**[September is the perfect time for Oktoberfest in Burlington](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/18/september-is-perfect-time-oktoberfest-burlington/)**

❝

*"Here you can at least compare and contrast so many different breweries and their styles," per WCAX.*

Oktoberfest returns tonight at 377 Pine Street, the South End Get Down lot, with more than 25 breweries pouring everything from traditional German beers to non alcoholic options, plus a traditional German band and other live performers. The games run from a stein holding competition and a keg toss to axe throwing and soccer darts, with prizes on the line. It goes 5:30 to 10 tonight and 4:30 to 9 on Saturday, and WCAX's Ike Bendavid is the master of ceremonies.

**[Lyric Theatre Launches Free Ticket Program](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/arts-culture/performingarts/lyric-theatre-launches-free-ticket-program)**

❝

*"For many people, going to a live performance is out of reach," per Seven Days.*

Lyric Theatre sold out all six performances of Frozen at the 1,400 seat Flynn, and now the community theater company wants people in those seats who could not otherwise afford one. Lyric for All launches this fall with 200 to 300 free tickets to the November show, Elf, distributed through partner nonprofits, with Lund and Mercy Connections among the six approached so far, plus tours of Lyric's South Burlington headquarters and workshops on costuming, poster design and staging. The program has collected more than $50,000. The seed money came from Girls Nite Out Productions, the Burlington theater company that is folding after 15 years and giving an estimated $30,000 to $40,000, including proceeds from its farewell show October 29 and 30 at Main Street Landing.

**[Jason Eaton tries to fire his attorneys midway through his attempted murder trial](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/17/jason-eaton-tries-to-fire-his-attorneys-midway-through-his-attempted-murder-trial)**

❝

*"I vigorously object to your denial," per VTDigger.*

Jason Eaton, the Burlington man facing three counts of attempted second degree murder for shooting three Palestinian college students on North Prospect Street in November 2023, stood up Thursday before jurors came in and asked Judge John Pacht to let him fire his public defenders and represent himself. Pacht said no. The state's expert, Boston forensic psychiatrist Dr. Jhilam Biswas, then testified that Eaton meets the criteria for a personality disorder but not a psychotic disorder, which would leave him short of Vermont's legal standard for insanity, rebutting the defense expert who testified a day earlier that he was legally insane. She described cannabis induced paranoia layered on the stress of a lost job and an apartment he was about to lose. Jury deliberations could begin as early as today.

**[Burlington City Council president considers run for mayor](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/17/burlington-city-council-president-considers-run-mayor)**

❝

*"And I think there are some changes that need to be made," per WCAX.*

City Council President Ben Traverse, a Ward 5 Democrat, says he is seriously considering a challenge to Progressive Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak if she seeks reelection, and he expects to decide in the coming weeks. The mayor said in a statement that it is good for democracy when people are interested in serving and that she will make her own reelection decision in the fall. The two are at odds over a public safety plan, which Traverse will discuss on WCAX's You Can Quote Me this Sunday at 11:30 AM.

**[Hundreds of layoffs announced at the 2 largest healthcare networks Vermonters rely on](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/15/hundreds-of-layoffs-announced-at-the-two-largest-healthcare-networks)**

❝

*"This is not the first or the last step we'll need to take to make our health system more sustainable," per VTDigger.*

UVM Health is laying off 199 people and Dartmouth Health is eliminating 124 jobs plus 303 open positions, both announced Tuesday ahead of the October 1 start of the hospital fiscal year. The UVM cuts are expected to save $16.8 million and reach all six hospitals in the network, with 118 of the positions at UVM Medical Center, where a general medicine unit closes and its patients move to a similarly equipped floor. Central Vermont Medical Center loses 24 positions, three mental health technicians among them, and plans to re-post 11 of those roles with changed responsibilities. The two unions estimate about 90 of their members are among those laid off, and the layoffs take effect by the end of October.

**[Vermont Regulators Order Hospital Price Cuts; UVM Hit Hardest](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/healthcare/vermont-regulators-order-hospital-price-cuts-uvm-hit-hardest)**

❝

*"I do think UVM is in the most fortunate position in the state with the largest reserves and can weather a bit of a reduction," per Seven Days.*

On Monday the Green Mountain Care Board approved budgets for all 14 Vermont hospitals and ordered UVM Medical Center to cut what it charges commercial insurers by 4.4%, the steepest cut in the state and well past both the board's own 1% guidance and the flat rate UVM requested. The vote was 3 to 2, with chair Owen Foster among the no votes. UVM Health CEO Dr. Stephen Leffler told the board the medical center expects to lose roughly $75 million this year and at least that much again next year, and that its reserves are holding up only because of the stock market. Hospitals account for nearly half of health care spending in Vermont against about a third nationally, and the new budgets take effect October 1.

**[UVM Health Union protests layoffs](https://www.mychamplainvalley.com/news/uvm-health-union-protests-layoffs)**

❝

*"They deserve you know, appropriate care and one on one time," per MyChamplainValley.*

The Vermont Federation of Nurses and Health Professionals and Support Staff United stepped out of an all day bargaining session with the hospital on Wednesday to protest the cuts, which include a management proposal to close a general medicine floor and an inpatient specialty care unit. Bridgett Mobbs, a registered nurse on Baird 3 who was laid off, said the hospital is already at max capacity and nurses already have too many patients. Employees whose positions are eliminated stay employed through a 45 day transition period with career support.

**[Burlington adds fencing, cameras and toilets at City Hall Park](https://www.mynbc5.com/article/burlington-adds-fencing-cameras-and-toilets-at-city-hall-park/73765716)**

❝

*"Mulvaney-Stanak said the cameras are not Flock cameras and do not use facial recognition technology or artificial intelligence," per NBC5.*

The city has fenced off two spots in City Hall Park, a ledge next to the Whiskey Room on College Street and an area behind the kiosk, where the mayor says the soil has become heavily compacted. It also placed temporary barriers along College Street to cut down on vehicle roll ins, added two ADA compliant portable toilets while the park's public restroom gets maintenance, and deployed mobile cameras for Burlington police.
