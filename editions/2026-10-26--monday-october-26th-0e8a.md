## Weather & Monday Update:

Keep a warm layer within reach this week, because the temperatures ride a roller coaster on the way to Halloween. Monday starts with some sun before clouds roll in ahead of a cold front, with a high near 57 and maybe a few sprinkles up north, though Burlington should stay mostly dry. Tuesday is sunny but sharply colder, topping out near 47 with a light north breeze, and Tuesday night drops to about 25, cold enough that a few spots around Vermont could see record lows Wednesday morning. Wednesday warms back up to a sunny 54, and Thursday is the pick of the week, mostly sunny and breezy from the south with a high near 63, before a chance of light showers Thursday night. Early looks at Halloween are dry and crisp, so plan on a jacket for trick or treating. Track it all week on my new weather page at [Burlington Right Now](https://guide.btownbrief.com/weather.html).

[Btown Brief Presents: Homeward Bound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLHBEUlTQPE)*****By Kwame Dankwa*

![](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/MLHBEUlTQPE/hqdefault.jpg)

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLHBEUlTQPE)

BURLINGTON, Vt. — Siobhan Neela-Stock, a freelance journalist, has spent her career telling other people's stories. Now she's about to tell her own; tracing her roots back to India, where she was adopted as a baby, and preparing for a search that will finally tell her who she came from. In [Kwame's 7 minute interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLHBEUlTQPE), Siobhan talks about being adopted by a white couple and growing up with three sisters also adopted from India, sometimes the only brown kids in their school, and about the year she finally went through her adoption papers and came away with more questions than answers, starting with her own birthday. She has since written about South Asian adoption for The Juggernaut and learned from other Indian adoptees that what the records say is not necessarily true.

Siobhan was born in India and adopted at a year and a half, and she grew up in Vermont. To find her birth family she has enlisted Arun Dohle and Anjali Pawar of the Adoptee Rights Council, which helps Indian adoptees find their biological families, and [her GoFundMe](https://www.gofundme.com/f/help-siobhan-find-her-birth-family-in-india) covers the first three years of search costs, with more than $3,100 raised toward its $11,268 goal so far, so give if you can, and share her story with anyone it might reach.

[Essex Experience Goes Up for Sale, but Longtime Owner Peter Edelmann Plans to Stay Involved](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/business-news/essex-experience-goes-up-for-sale-but-longtime-owner-peter-edelmann-plans-to-stay-involved)

❝

*"I've been a shepherd long enough," per Seven Days.*

Edelmann, 73, who turned a dying outlet mall into a hub of locally owned shops, cafés and makers, has listed the shopping center for $27.75 million, but he'll keep Essex Cinemas, which he plans to upgrade, and the Double E performance center. **[Desjardin inspires Seahorses soccer team](https://www.wcax.com/2026/10/20/desjardin-inspires-seahorses)**

❝

*"And what this young lady has gone through, our team just rallied around her," per WCAX.*

Mariella Desjardin is one of just a couple of freshmen on Burlington High School's varsity girls soccer team, and she's playing through kidney disease that has now put her on the deceased donor list for a transplant, so the call could come at any time. **[Vermont experts say bats need our help. How do we get over our fear of them?](https://vtdigger.org/2026/10/20/vermont-experts-say-bats-need-our-help-how-do-we-get-over-our-fear-of-them)**

❝

*"People who were like, 'Bats are gross and scary,' said, 'I still think they're gross and scary, but I know they're in trouble, and I don't want to hurt them,'" per VTDigger.*

It's International Bat Week, a good excuse to get to know the nine bat species that call Vermont home, five of which are listed as threatened or endangered, and white nose syndrome has killed nearly 6 million bats across the Northeast since 2006. Milton's Barry Genzlinger, known as Barry the Bat Guy, estimates he has rehabilitated at least 1,000 bats and keeps the ones people find in their homes over winter safe until he can release them in spring. If you find a bat inside in the middle of winter, don't toss it out in the snow, call the Vermont Bat Center instead. This is also the time of year bats look for a place to hibernate, so Fish & Wildlife has guidance for safely keeping them out of your attic.

**['Vermont is not a climate refuge': UVM researchers release new climate assessment](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-10-23/vermont-not-climate-refuge-uvm-researchers-release-new-climate-assessment)**

❝

*"Our aging infrastructure, aging population and rural mountainous geography make us more vulnerable to climate impacts," per Vermont Public.*

UVM's third Vermont Climate Assessment, released Friday, finds the state warming faster than the global average, with Burlington now seeing triple the days above 90 degrees it did in 2020 while just a third of Vermont households have air conditioning. The surface of Lake Champlain is on average 7 degrees warmer than in the 1960s, fueling the cyanobacteria blooms that regularly close the only beaches Burlingtonians can walk to. One bright spot is skiing, which the last assessment questioned by century's end, since researchers now expect ski areas to have the water they need for snowmaking.

**[Bernie Sanders Hits the Campaign Trail With Amanda Janoo in Winooski](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/election-2026/bernie-sanders-hits-the-campaign-trail-with-amanda-janoo-in-winooski)**

❝

*"This is about making health care free at the point of care, while building a publicly funded system that is accountable to the people it serves," per Seven Days.*

Sanders joined the Democratic/Progressive candidate for governor outside the Community Health Centers in Winooski, his first time campaigning publicly alongside her since endorsing her on Oct. 7, to champion her plan for publicly funded primary and preventive care. Janoo estimates the program would cost at least $320 million a year and says higher taxes on wealthy Vermonters could raise roughly $260 million of it, while Gov. Phil Scott argued in their debate that Vermont can't afford another experiment like that and it has to be done nationally.

**[Vermont weighs new tax on high earners amid budget pressures](https://www.wcax.com/2026/10/21/vermont-weighs-new-tax-high-earners-amid-budget-pressures)**

❝

*"What we have is folks who are running on national political rhetoric promising to tax oligarchs, when all we have to tax is oncologists," per WCAX.*

The Fair Share campaign, backed by Janoo and Molly Gray, claims raising taxes on households making $250,000 or more could bring in $345 million a year for primary care, with an extra 2% on some 22,000 households under half a million and an extra 8% on about 4,500 households above it. The Lake Champlain Chamber of Commerce says those households already pay more than a third of Vermont's income tax and worries they'll leave, while the Public Assets Institute's Anika Heilweil says many higher earners are older and not as mobile. Scott, if reelected, would almost certainly veto it.
