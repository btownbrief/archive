## Weather & Monday Update:

If you can sneak your outdoor plans into the morning today, do it. ## Local News (All Links Clickable)

**[After a nearly six year saga, Burlington's new high school opens its doors](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/21/after-a-nearly-six-year-saga-burlingtons-new-high-school-opens-its-doors/)**

❝

*"I'm really loving it, but I know I'm gonna get lost for the first few weeks of school... I feel like I can breathe," per VTDigger.*

Burlington High School's new building on the former New North End campus is ready for students, closing the saga that started when PCB contamination surfaced in 2020 and pushed classes into a hastily renovated downtown Macy's. The rebuild came in around $206 million, most of it covered by the $165 million bond voters approved in 2022 plus $16 million from the state, and students touring the halls kept coming back to the natural light after years in a windowless department store where science rooms had no running water and gym class meant a walk to the YMCA. The district's lawsuit against Monsanto is expected to go to trial in early 2027, and as of January 171 schools statewide still had not done their mandated PCB testing. Welcome home, Seahorses.

**[New funds for Alburgh rail bridge could boost future Amtrak link to Montreal](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/17/new-funds-for-alburgh-rail-bridge-could-boost-future-amtrak-link-to-montreal/)**

❝

*"Having that Canadian connection would be just one more enticement to bring our Canadian visitors back," per VTDigger.*

The U.S. DOT awarded $4 million to plan repairs or replacement of the century old East Alburgh Bridge, the Lake Champlain crossing so worn that trains cross it at 5 miles per hour. The bridge sits on the route that carried Amtrak's Montrealer between Washington and Montreal until 1995, and rail advocates say restoring the Quebec link has fresh urgency with Canadian visitors staying home. The money covers planning only, and the bigger hurdle is still the customs preclearance facility at Montreal Central Station, which remains on the drawing board.

[Veterans Affairs to open clinic in South Burlington](https://www.vtcng.com/otherpapersbvt/news/local_news/veterans-affairs-to-open-clinic-in-south-burlington/article_1e30b0b2-b3c5-428e-80ad-d1c58fa35163.html)

❝

*"We're anticipating that this is really going to improve access for veterans in all of Northwestern Vermont," per The Other Paper.*

The VA signed a 20 year lease for a new outpatient clinic at Technology Park in South Burlington, expected to open by 2030 and replace the current Burlington clinic. The new spot puts roughly 7,900 veterans within an hour's drive, and the 24,000 square foot space adds mental health services plus physical and occupational therapy to the primary care, optometry and audiology already offered. Officials also like the practical stuff, parking, highway access, a bus line, and a location just down the street from the Vet Center on Gregory Drive.

**[Vermont Air National Guard deployment stretches to 8 months](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-08-19/vermont-air-national-guard-deployment-stretches-8-months-no-return)**

❝

*"This is a federal issue. They don't talk with us much about what they're doing and what they're not doing," per Vermont Public.*

Members of the 158th Fighter Wing and their F-35s have been deployed on back to back missions since December, first the Caribbean and then the Middle East, with no announced return date and roughly 200 members still overseas. Guard members typically deploy for six months every three years and usually know when they are coming home, which is what makes this stretch so unusual for the families waiting on it. The congressional delegation wrote the defense secretary this summer over hazard pay and benefits concerns, and Sen. Welch's office says the immediate pay issues have been resolved.

**[Sunset Lake Cannabis Goes Green by Farming Only Outdoors](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/guides/cannabeat/sunset-lake-cannabis-goes-green-by-farming-only-outdoors/)**

❝

*"Come here on a Saturday. We could be an AARP ad," per Seven Days.*

Sam Bellavance, who learned to farm on his family's Alburgh dairy, grows every ounce of Sunset Lake Cannabis outdoors in South Hero, no grow lights, parasitic wasps for pest control, and one of only four Sun+Earth certifications in the state. The bet has made him one of Vermont's biggest outdoor growers at 1,000 to 1,500 pounds of trimmed flower a year, with sales climbing from under $100,000 in 2022 to several million this year. His dispensary Lake Effect just moved into a long vacant Route 2 pharmacy, where a customer survey pegged the average buyer at 58 years old.

**[Artisan cannabis cultivators are struggling to make money in Vermont](https://vtcommunitynews.org/2026/08/18/artisan-cannabis-cultivators-are-struggling-to-make-money-in-vermont/)**

❝

*"I'm getting crushed by this industry. It is crushing my soul. It is financially not viable," per Community News Service.*

The flip side of that success story. Four years into legal sales, small growers without retail licenses say the market is squeezing them out, since only retailers can sell to consumers, nearly half of the state's 110 cannabis retailers grow their own product, and the Cannabis Control Board froze new retail licenses last year. Underhill grower Sara Farnsworth says at least 10 growers have told her they may not renew their licenses, and she has co-founded a cooperative pushing for direct to consumer sales at farmers markets and by delivery. The control board says it supports direct sales and has recommended $1 million in state support for small cultivators, but the legislature has to act first.

**[Police roll out a squad of new two-wheeled cruisers](https://www.vtcng.com/otherpapersbvt/news/police_blotter/police-roll-out-a-squad-of-new-two-wheeled-cruisers/article_bf556c9a-2f45-4b94-b6d4-d1f11a3e2fc7.html)**

❝

*"If they do have to take somebody into custody, obviously another officer has to come and transport them in a car. They're not putting them on the handlebars," per The Other Paper.*

South Burlington police launched a bike patrol this month, three mountain bikes and two e-bikes focused on the growing City Center area, with officers in bright blue polos. Deputy chief Sean Briscoe trained on bike patrol in Saratoga Springs in the late 1990s and taught the course himself, covering everything from braking to when to dismount in a pursuit. The aim is visibility and connection rather than arrests, with patrols rolling out in daytime hours for now.

**[Vermont's statewide ban on cellphones in schools starts this year](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/23/vermonts-statewide-ban-on-cellphones-in-schools-starts-this-year/)**

❝

*"We've had overwhelming positive responses from families, from students, from teachers," per VTDigger.*

Vermont's ban on student cellphone use during the school day takes full effect this year, and districts must now have policies at least as strict as the state's model. Enforcement varies from lockers to magnetic pouches to plain old backpacks, and districts that started early, like Lamoille South, report less cyberbullying and better attention and achievement. Advocates argue backpack rules do not go far enough since research shows a nearby phone still pulls focus, and one student described classmates breaking into locked pouches and talking to Siri through them. Advocacy group VT-IRL.org is grading schools statewide and reserves an A for schools that store phones centrally all day.

**[BETA releases 2Q 2026 results, revenues more than double](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2026/august/19/beta-releases-2q-2026-results-revenues-more-double)**

❝

*"This quarter showed that the investments made across the business continue to translate into real-world operations and delivered tangible results," per Vermont Business Magazine.*

South Burlington's BETA Technologies reported second quarter revenue of $14.7 million, more than double a year ago, against a net loss of $148.8 million as it keeps spending toward scale. The quarter had real milestones, the first operations under the FAA's eVTOL pilot program flying manufactured organs with United Therapeutics, the world's first hybrid electric flight above 30,000 feet, and a charging network now at 138 sites. Scotland's Loganair also signed a term sheet for five aircraft with options for five more.

**[From hobbit holes to giant sculptures, Joplin Wistar turns imagination into local landmarks](https://vtcommunitynews.org/2026/08/14/from-hobbit-holes-to-giant-sculptures-joplin-wistar-turns-imagination-into-local-landmarks/)**

❝

*"He's incapable of doing anything the same way twice. He doesn't even like to take the same road or the same trail when we're hiking or something," per Community News Service.*

Joplin Wistar, a retired Shelburne Community School teacher of 30 years, has spent his retirement filling Shelburne with hand built whimsy, a backyard hobbit hole, a river troll named Strömkarlen guarding the LaPlatte Crossing footbridge, and Zephyr, a 16 foot wooden Greek wind god installed at Shelburne Pond Studios in June. He never charges for the work, builds mostly from grapevine and recycled pallet wood, and aims for one new project a year. Next up, he is pitching a 20 foot web structure that would project the northern lights for this year's Night Fires performance.

**[Upstate Elevator Built Its Business By Adapting, It Now Faces Another Test](https://mountaintimes.info/2026/08/19/upstate-elevator-built-its-business-by-adapting-it-now-faces-another-test/)**

❝

*"Uncertainty is a tax you pay for being early into something. We built this business that doesn't depend on Washington getting it right," per Mountain Times.*

Burlington's Upstate Elevator has grown since 2017 into a CBD and cannabis company with 30 employees, beverages in 15 states and products at about 1,000 retailers. Now federal restrictions taking effect November 12 would cap consumable hemp products at 0.4 mg of THC per container, which would effectively ban many of the hemp derived products the company depends on. Founder Dylan Raap says he will reformulate if it comes to that and is already developing products from other botanicals like kanna and kava, and in the meantime the company just swept the cannabis category at the Seven Daysies with four wins.

**[Stuck in Vermont: Boating With Michael Hendrickson of Burlington Harbor Launch and Tow](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/arts-culture/stuckinvermont/stuck-in-vermont-boating-with-michael-hendrickson-of-burlington-harbor-launch-and-tow/)**

❝

*"Every day at some point, I'm on the water out here on this lake, and I have a moment where I look around and I'm like, I'm pretty lucky that I get to be on my very favorite body of water, doing the work... that I love," per Seven Days.*

The latest Stuck in Vermont rides along with Michael "Spike" Hendrickson, who grew up sailing and scuba diving on Lake Champlain and started Burlington Harbor Launch and Tow in 2019 after decades in restaurants and bars, with paramedic work still filling his winters. His five boats, all ladies, Big Mae, Darla, Little Belle among them, handle marine towing, salvage, commercial diving and dock installs, and from April 1 to December 1 he does not stray far from the waterfront, dozens of groundings a year included. In the episode, Eva Sollberger tags along as his crew swaps broken tire joints on the Community Sailing Center's floating breakwater, then races a squall back to Burlington.

**[Vermont Senior Games pickleball tournament sets record-breaking numbers](https://www.mynbc5.com/article/vermont-senior-games-pickleball-tournament-sets-record-breaking-numbers/73504974)**

## UVM Athletics: Four Different Scorers Power a 4-1 Colgate Rout as the Homestand Rolls On

No. 13 men's soccer [beat Colgate 4-1 at Virtue Field on Sunday](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/8/23/mens-soccer-13-mens-soccer-defeats-colgate-4-1-behind-four-different-goalscorers.aspx) in the Peter Baldwin Memorial Game, with four different Catamounts scoring and three of them notching their first career UVM goal. Luis Fischer opened in the 13th minute off a long ball from Islam Houssni, Houssni buried a left footed shot in the 27th off a Noel Bjork set piece, David Ismail knocked in his third of the season off a Fischer corner in the 70th, and Philipp Schmidtke found the top corner in the 79th after a Thaddeus Liquori backheel before Colgate got one back late. Houssni and Fischer each finished with a goal and an assist, Ismail's five shots on goal were a career high, and Niklas Herceg made four saves as Vermont moved to 2-0. Head coach Adrian Dubois said he was very pleased with the collective performance against a strong, competitive Colgate side, and the win triggered a $100 Vermont Mutual donation to the Ronald McDonald House. The four match homestand continues Friday at 6 against [Charlotte on ESPN+](https://uvmathletics.com/sports/mens-soccer/schedule/2026).

Women's soccer [fell 3-0 at Army on Sunday afternoon](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/8/23/womens-soccer-falls-to-army-on-the-road-sunday-afternoon.aspx), scoreless at the half before the Black Knights scored three times in the second half. Sophia Kling was busy again in goal, Eleni Stowell led the team with four shots, and the Catamounts, now 0-3-1, close their road trip [at Stonehill on Sunday](https://uvmathletics.com/sports/womens-soccer/schedule/2026) before finally opening at home September 3 against Boston College. Field hockey, the America East preseason favorite, [opens its season Friday at 3 against Northeastern](https://uvmathletics.com/sports/field-hockey/schedule/2026) at Moulton Winder Field.
