## Weather & Weekend Rundown:

Hang in there through a damp Friday morning, because this weekend is the payoff. A cold front sweeps the leftover showers and mugginess out by early afternoon, the sun takes over, and we top out in the mid 70s with a crisp night in the 40s to follow. Saturday is the day to build everything around, wall to wall sunshine near 78 with low humidity and zero rain in the forecast. Sunday runs warmer near 79 and starts sunny, then showers and storms become possible after 2, so lean toward morning and early afternoon for the outdoor stuff. Track it all week, with patio, swim, and sunset scores, at [Burlington Right Now](https://guide.btownbrief.com/weather.html).

The fair is back! The [Champlain Valley Fair](https://www.helloburlingtonvt.com/event/champlain-valley-fair/7782/) opens its ten day run today at the Expo in Essex Junction, gates at noon, and opening day is a deal day with kids 5 to 12 getting in for $5. Country riser [Warren Zeiders ($39-69)](https://champlainvalleyfair.org/event/warren-zeiders/champlain-valley-fair/essex-junction-vermont/) opens the grandstand concert series tonight at 7 with gates at 5:30. Back in town, the legendary Glover troupe Bread & Puppet brings [The Upside-Down World Circus](https://www.flynnvt.org/Events/2026/8/Bread-Puppet-Theatre) to the Flynn Main Stage at 7, stilt dancers, giant papier-mâché beasts and a riotous brass band, with their famous sourdough rye served after the show. Comedy people have options, with YouTube comedian Alex Clark polishing his next special at [Jokes From The Brink](https://www.helloburlingtonvt.com/event/alex-clark-%7c-jokes-from-the-brink-%7c-stand-up-comedy/7166/) at Off Center at 7:30, 20% of profits going to Dad Guild, and a free [All That Jazz Comedy Showcase](https://community.sevendaysvt.com/event/all-that-jazz-comedy-showcase-45389357) in the basement jazz bar at The 126 at 7. Early birds could’ve joined the [Skirack x August First run club](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcjJscFuv3G/) at 7 AM for a social 3 to 4 miles on the bike path with coffee after, so hit that up next week! After work, the [South End Get Down](https://community.sevendaysvt.com/event/south-end-get-down-45175750) brings the food trucks and DJs back to Coal Collective from 5 to 9 for the second to last time this season, and [Moonbird](https://www.switchbackvt.com/beer-garden-events/music-at-the-beer-garden-moonbird) plays a free set in Switchback's beer garden from 5:30 to 8:30. The night side belongs to dancers and tribute crowds, with the [Lake Champlain Chamber Music Festival](https://lccmf.org/2026schedule) playing Elley-Long at 7:30 with concerts running through Sunday, Tragically Hip tribute [The Strictly Hip ($25)](https://highergroundmusic.com/event/the-strictly-hip/) in the Higher Ground Ballroom, free [Dancing in the Streets with Blues for Breakfast](https://www.skinnypancake.com/events/) on the Skinny Pancake waterfront patio from 8 to 11, and the 90s and 2000s rave [Better Off Alone](https://highergroundmusic.com/event/better-off-alone/) in the Showcase Lounge with doors at 8:30.

Saturday is stacked, and two of its best things are completely free. [A_DOG DAY No. 13](https://www.friendsforadog.org/ffad-events/adog-day-no13-2026) takes over the waterfront skatepark from 12 to 6, the annual skate and music day honoring Andy "A_Dog" Williams, the beloved Burlington DJ and skater the park is named for. Thirteen years on it is the closest thing this city has to a hometown holiday, DJs going all afternoon, skating everywhere you look, and this year's merch designed by Stefan Echo. Down in Shelburne, [Burlington Taiko celebrates 40 years](https://burlingtontaiko.org/upcoming-events-performances/) of thunderous Japanese drumming with a free afternoon at the Shelburne Farms Coach Barn from 1 to 5, rain or shine. Full performances at 1 and 4 sandwich a 2:30 workshop where anyone can get on the drums, registration encouraged but not required. Our own [BTown Coffee Club [MEETUP]](https://www.meetup.com/burlington-social-activites-group/events/316044386/) holds it down at Zero Gravity at 10, so grab a coffee and come say hi. Market lovers get a double header with the [Burlington Vintage Market](https://www.instagram.com/p/DchH5eBFkWJ/) running its final summer edition at Barge Canal from 9 to 2 and Love Burlington's [BTV Market](https://loveburlington.org/btvmarket) filling City Hall Park with 25 plus makers from 11 to 3. At the fair, [Jordan Davis ($39-69)](https://champlainvalleyfair.org/event/jordan-davis/champlain-valley-fair/essex-junction-vermont/) headlines the grandstand at 7 with Matt Lang, and the Green Mountain Mashers judge the [12th annual Champlain Valley Fair Homebrew Competition](https://www.facebook.com/events/904819225261737/) at noon, 28 entries deep this year. Gamers can grab a morning seat at [Board Game Brunch [MEETUP]](https://www.meetup.com/friendlytabletopgamers/events/316032669/) at Old Town Cafe & Comics in Williston from 9 to 12, and anyone craving a slower start can try [Pause & Play](https://www.instagram.com/p/DcdrL_eiR-t/), a no experience needed art and play morning at Play Intention from 9 to 11:30, registration required. In the afternoon, Bollywood classic [Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge](https://www.helloburlingtonvt.com/event/dilwale-dulhania-le-jayenge/7826/) screens at VTIFF at 3. Saturday night, Lebanese born comic [Nataly Aukar ($25)](https://www.helloburlingtonvt.com/event/nataly-aukar-at-vt-comedy-club/7834/) plays 7 and 9 at Vermont Comedy Club, local death metal outfit Split in Half says goodbye with [one final show](https://highergroundmusic.com/event/split-in-half-final-show/) in the Showcase Lounge with doors at 7, and [It's A 2000's Party](https://highergroundmusic.com/event/its-a-2000s-party-burlington/) turns the Ballroom into a Y2K throwback at 9.

Sunday belongs to Winooski. The [Fiber Fair](https://downtownwinooski.org/winooski-farmers-market/fiber-fair) takes over the farmers market from 10 to 2 with a dozen fiber arts vendors, live sheep petting, labor union songs from Rik Palieri and open history museums in the mill, and then the [Winooski Friendship Festival](https://www.instagram.com/p/DcjAWaKjGzp/) runs 2 to 9 at the school on Normand Street with Kenyan born jazz vocalist KeruBo, the African and Bhutanese Nepali dance troupe, a community soccer tournament and free trolley rides around town. Just get the outdoor plans in early, since storms become possible late afternoon. Shelburne Orchards hosts the fifth annual [Harvest Festival ($25, kids free)](https://community.sevendaysvt.com/event/harvest-festival-45421573) benefiting Food 4 Farmers from 3 to 6 with live music, wood fired pizza and tractor rides, the [VT Synth Society takes over Foam](https://www.helloburlingtonvt.com/event/vt-synth-society%3a-synth-night/7876/) for a free night of experimental electronics from 5 to 8, and crossover thrash legends [D.R.I. ($15-20)](https://highergroundmusic.com/event/d-r-i/) hit the Showcase Lounge with doors at 6:30. Crafty folks can drop into [Crafternoons](https://www.facebook.com/events/1035886642447293/) at the Odd Fellows hall on North Ave at 3. And a heads up on the fair's grandstand, the Sunday 4 PM [Monster Truck: A Night of Fire & Destruction](https://champlainvalleyfair.org/event/monster-truck-2/champlain-valley-fair/essex-junction-vermont/) show is already showing sold out on the fair's page, so if you don't have tickets, plan around it.

## Local News (All Links Clickable)

[Burlington Named the #3 Best US City to Work](https://www.travelandleisure.com/25-best-us-cities-for-work-indeed-analysis-12066166)

❝

*"[Burlington] gets there not on big-city pay but on the highest job-opportunity score in the country—an exceptionally tight labor market where job seekers have real leverage—paired with strong, broadly shared outcomes across groups," per Travel + Leisure.*

An Indeed study of metro areas over 200,000 ranked Burlington third in the country for work, behind only Boston and Washington DC and ahead of San Jose and San Francisco. The ranking weighed six pillars, from compensation to work-life balance, and our win came on job opportunity, where apparently Burlington posted the highest score in the nation. That’s an interesting one I didn’t expect, you agree?

[‘Watershed moment’: Vermont secures $92.7M and new restrictions for teens in Meta settlement](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/26/watershed-moment-vermont-secures-92-7m-and-new-restrictions-for-teens-in-meta-settlement/)

❝

*"Vermont's children will be healthier, and Vermont's future will be brighter," per VTDigger.*

Attorney General Charity Clark's 2023 lawsuit against Meta ended this week in the largest single-company settlement in state history, $92.7 million plus a decade of required protections for users under 18, including a nighttime block, a 2 hour daily limit, better age verification and a school mode that quiets notifications during the day. The deal was negotiated alongside 51 other states and territories, with the national total expected to top $17 billion. Vermont punched above its weight here, since Meta fought all the way to the Supreme Court to argue it couldn't be sued in Chittenden County and lost. Clark is pursuing similar claims against TikTok, YouTube and Snap, and the first changes are due within weeks.

[Burlington Police arrest 11 people during month of drug operations in City Hall Park](https://www.mynbc5.com/article/burlington-vermont-city-hall-park-drug-busts/73543300)

❝

*"These are not overt police officers; they're operating covertly, in plainclothes, with a lot of surveillance using technology," per NBC5.*

Burlington Police worked with the DEA and ATF on a multi-week covert operation in City Hall Park that produced 11 drug-related arrests between August 6 and 26, with plainclothes officers watching the park and its edges all month. Interim chief Shawn Burke wants to sustain the operations and tied that to the department's push for adequate staffing. The follow-through now shifts to the courts, prosecutors and treatment providers.

[Burlington Asks Again for State Help With Public Safety Problems](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/burlington-asks-again-for-state-help-with-public-safety-problems)

❝

*"It is crucial for our city government to stand united to advocate for more immediate and stronger state action," per Seven Days.*

In a rare moment of consensus, the City Council passed a unanimous resolution alongside Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak asking Gov. Phil Scott for immediate help on homelessness and public safety. The asks include support for a new 100-bed shelter in Chittenden County, faster access to the $83 million the Legislature set aside for emergency shelter overhaul, reversing the planned 70-day annual limit on the motel housing program, reopened mental health beds and more State Police support. Scott said he was "disappointed" by the requests and has already declined to extend the troopers' recent downtown patrols, so the gap between city and state remains wide. The mayor hoped to meet Scott before Labor Day, while he said in the next month or two.

[Burlington artist dug Nap pleads not guilty in death of teen cyclist](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/27/burlington-artist-dug-nap-struck-and-killed-e-bike-rider-in-crosswalk-new-court-filing-alleges/)

❝

*"It is reasonable to believe an attentive driver would have noticed the amber LED lights engaged and upon observing the cyclist in the crosswalk, come to a stop prior to crashing into them," per VTDigger.*

Douglas Knapp, 79, the folk artist known around town as dug Nap, pleaded not guilty Thursday to negligent operation with death resulting in the July crash that killed 16 year old Shiloh Skalka, a rising junior at Burlington High School riding her e-bike. The newly public affidavit says Skalka activated the flashing crosswalk signal at Pine and Locust, waited about 3 seconds, and was struck by Knapp's van, which video showed holding a steady 28 mph in the 25 zone with no braking until just before impact. Police calculated he had about 150 feet to stop, per [Seven Days](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/teen-cyclist-was-in-a-crosswalk-marked-by-flashing-lights-when-fatally-struck-by-vehicle). Skalka's death drew a vigil ride of hundreds earlier this month, and her parents attended Thursday's hearing with a group of fellow cyclists. Knapp was released on the condition that he not drive, with the next hearing October 27. If convicted, Knapp faces up to two years in jail.

[New students arrive at UVM as city, campus step up safety measures](https://www.wcax.com/2026/08/26/new-students-arrive-uvm-city-campus-step-up-safety-measures/)

❝

*"We want them to feel like this is their town. It's been our town for our entire life as a university, and as we support Burlington, we support our students," per WCAX.*

UVM move-in is underway, and both the university and the city are leaning into safety this year, with required safety modules before arrival, the CatSafe app connecting students to campus police or BPD around the clock, and a resource list from the mayor's office that includes a drug trafficking tipline. The first-years WCAX talked to said they feel safe on campus and plan to keep their heads up downtown.

[Halfway through Vermont's first ever Navy Week](https://www.mychamplainvalley.com/top-stories/halfway-through-vermonts-first-ever-navy-week/)

❝

*"There's such rich history here from a NAVY perspective. Even though there's no NAVY here. Some very important battles fought here. Some of our greatest naval heroes here right up the lake, right?" per MyChamplainValley.*

Burlington Navy Week rolls on through Sunday, with ship demonstrations, live drills and daily Navy band music on the waterfront, plus a Navy presence at the fair starting today. Fewer than 100 active sailors call Vermont home, but the lake's naval history runs deep, and two vessels carry our city names, the fast-attack submarine USS Montpelier and the support ship USNS Burlington, currently deployed near South America.

[Rough pavement on I-89 caused by "raveling," VTrans says](https://www.wcax.com/2026/08/28/rough-pavement-i-89-caused-by-raveling-vtrans-says/)

❝

*"What you're seeing is basically, as that pavement structure starts to deteriorate, it basically actually disintegrates and comes apart. And what you end up traveling on in the worst case scenario is the layer below it," per WCAX.*

If you've felt your car wander in the right lane of I-89 between Montpelier and St. Albans, there's now a name for it. VTrans chief engineer Jeremy Reed says those grooves come from raveling rather than rutting, the top layer of pavement disintegrating so that in the worst spots you're driving on the layer underneath. The likely culprits are heavier traffic and a thin preservation treatment laid down 5 to 8 years ago that has reached the end of its life. Drivers told WCAX the grooves fill with water and hydroplane terribly in the rain, worth remembering with holiday weekend traffic ahead. VTrans says it monitors conditions annually, patches anything that becomes a hazard and is experimenting with pavement mixes that ravel less, though no repaving timeline was offered.

[15 years after Irene, plucky business owners recall the monster storm and its aftermath](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/24/15-years-after-irene-plucky-business-owners-recall-the-monster-storm-and-its-aftermath/)

❝

*"Irene showed us how vulnerable a small town can be but it also showed us just how strong Wilmington is when people take care of each other," per VTDigger.*

Fifteen years ago today, Tropical Storm Irene sent rivers over their banks across Vermont, and this remembrance from Wilmington captures what that Sunday cost. Bartleby's Books took 3.5 feet of water and lost 85% of its inventory, roughly $400,000 in damage with less than half insured, and the flood line on Town Hall still sits a few inches above the 1938 record. The through line in every story is how fast the town showed up for itself, neighbors covering car payments and shoveling mud until businesses reopened. The bookstore now keeps empty boxes on the third floor, just in case.

[Vermont's bear population is booming. So is conflict with humans](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-08-26/bear-population-booming-conflict-with-humans)

❝

*"Seeing an increase in the number of bears killed illegally in conflict situations is a red flag, right? Like that's one of those red flags that we might be starting to push up beyond public tolerance for this animal on the landscape," per Vermont Public.*

Vermont's game wardens have fielded roughly 1,800 bear calls so far this year, on pace for a record, and one bear in Jamaica ransacked a house and left the stove burner on. The population has more than tripled over 50 years to an estimated 7,500 to 8,900 animals, about double the state's target, so Fish and Wildlife wants to extend bear season into mid-December, allow two bears per hunter in parts of the state, and ban killing sows with cubs. Wildlife advocates counter that more hunting doesn't reliably reduce conflicts and want stricter food storage rules instead. Public comment runs through September 4, and in the meantime the advice is the usual, secure the trash, take down the feeders and fence the coops.

[Vital Communities enters talks to save town listservs from shutdown](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/26/vital-communities-enters-talks-to-save-town-listservs-from-shutdown/)

❝

*"I am surprised that Vital Communities did not recognize the value to the community and decided to shut them down in such an abrupt manner," per VTDigger.*

The 38 town listservs of the Upper Valley got a stay of execution. After Vital Communities announced it would stop supporting the two decade old email lists, community backlash bought a delay, and a group of residents and list managers is now forming a nonprofit called UV Lists to take them over this fall, with subscribers promised the same emails from a new domain for about $10,000 a year in tech costs. These lists carry town news, lost pets and business deals, and once matched a kidney donor with a recipient.
