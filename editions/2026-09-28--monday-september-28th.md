## Weather & Monday Update:

The nor'easter is on its way out, but Monday stays gray with showers on and off, most likely through the afternoon and into dinnertime, and a high around 62 before things wind down tonight. Tuesday could hang onto a shower in the morning before turning drier by afternoon, with a northerly breeze, highs in the low to mid 60s and clouds that may be stubborn. A south wind warms things up from there, with Wednesday partly sunny near 72 and Thursday the warmest day of the week at 77 and breezy, before a cold front brings a chance of showers Friday and a cooler, mostly sunny Saturday. Track it all week, with patio, swim, and sunset scores, at [Burlington Right Now](https://guide.btownbrief.com/weather.html).

Monday gets going at Fletcher Free Library, where the weekly [Shakespeare Out Loud](https://fletcherfree.libcal.com/event/17497809) group finishes reading Romeo and Juliet from 4 to 5:30, no experience needed and listeners welcome. At 6, UVM professor Ken Allen talks through the causes of [Vermont's healthcare crisis](https://frontporchforum.com/calendar/events/518628) and his case for single payer at the Burlington Friends Meeting House on North Prospect Street. Bird people can head to Phoenix Books on Church Street at 7, where Oregon author Marina Richie launches [Feathered Forest](https://sevendaystickets.com/e/marina-richie-with-trish-o-kane-feathered-forest-a) in conversation with Vermont birder Trish O'Kane, free with an RSVP. [Ora Cogan with Alice Grace](https://www.eventbrite.com/e/ora-cogan-w-alice-grace-tickets-2000264539082) over at Foam Brewers with doors at 7 and haunted folk and shadowy country at 7:30. [Betting the farm on 8 sunny days: Inside Vermont’s pick-your-own orchards](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/25/vermont-pick-your-own-orchards-success-depends-on-the-weather)

❝

*"At a certain point, people need to pick apples," per VTDigger.*

At Chapin Orchard in Essex Junction, the Murdock family spends money on its trees all year and has about eight days to earn it back, since pick your own season comes down to four weekends from late September into early October. Even the forecast matters, and on a recent Sunday when rain was forecast but never fell, Phil Murdock says Chapin did half of Saturday's business. UVM's Terry Bradshaw calls it a generally good year for apples despite an April frost, and Murdock plans to keep pick your own going right through the end of October.

[Are Vermont shopping malls making a comeback?](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/21/are-vermont-shopping-malls-making-comeback)

❝

*"I think arguably it’s the most developable parcel in Vermont," per WCAX.*

That is South Burlington City Manager Jessie Baker on the University Mall, where one worker says weekends still get crazy even as experts point to the need for investment since Sears left. Mall values are up 13% nationally according to The Wall Street Journal, and Baker says the owners have a master plan to build a grid of blocks connecting the property to Garden Street and Market Street, with more people living, working and shopping there. What happens to the mall building itself is still unknown.

[Students dive into tech at Burlington’s Hack Club](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/25/students-dive-into-tech-burlingtons-hack-club)

❝

*"By the time they graduate, we want them to have a sense of agency. They get that sense of agency by producing real-world work," per WCAX.*

Champlain Valley School District students are building their own projects at Hack Club in Burlington, from coding and websites to 3D printing, through the state's Flexible Pathways program. Student Miles Bergendahl works as one of the club's engineers, designing projects for other students to take on, and co-founder Christina Asquith says kids who are excited about what they are building keep learning after school. Hack Club says it welcomes more schools to get involved.

[Vermont Supreme Court allows Gov. Scott to enforce in-person work for state employees](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/25/in-reversal-vermont-supreme-court-allows-gov-scott-to-enforce-in-person-work-for-state-employees)

❝

*"The Governor’s assessment of the operating needs of his administration was reasonable, and it is not up to this Court to assess the wisdom of that decision," per VTDigger.*

The Supreme Court's unanimous Friday ruling reverses an April 1 Labor Relations Board order, so Gov. Phil Scott can keep requiring state employees in the office at least three days a week, the policy in place since December. The state employees' union called it "a great disappointment," and executive director Steve Howard warns that without affordable housing, childcare and transportation some workers may leave, though early data showed little sign of large departures. The union says it will now be "redoubling our efforts to elect Amanda Janoo" ahead of the November 3 election.

[ICE Surge Is Targeting Vermont's Roofing and Construction Workers](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/immigration-news/ice-surge-is-targeting-vermonts-roofing-and-construction-workers)

❝

*"Then, when I do, ICE comes. It’s crazy," per Seven Days.*

Mary Richards of Cornwall spent three years looking for someone to replace her leaking slate roof, and on the crew's second day in August, ICE agents detained two roofers from a South Burlington company in less than 10 minutes. Migrant Justice counts at least 30 roofing and construction workers detained at 13 worksite raids and traffic stops in Vermont so far this year, including three Morristown roofers hired by Five Star Roofing, which has offices in Shelburne. Federal agents have largely avoided raiding the dairy farms where migrant workers live, while construction crews work in plain view, which is how an agent driving past Richards' house spotted this one. The company sent another crew that same afternoon to finish the job, and neighbors shared pizzas, stood watch and some even climbed onto the roof to help lay shingles.

[Burlington High School class of 1966 tours new BHS](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/26/burlington-high-school-class-1966-tours-new-bhs)

❝

*"And I’m glad to see there is still a hill, cause that’s where we tended to win meets, was on that hill," per WCAX.*

The Class of 1966 was only the second to graduate from the old North Avenue building, and a reunion this weekend brought them back to tour the new BHS standing where it used to be. Richard Racusen, a 1965 cross country state champion with classmate Wayne Wilcox, said moving out of the old Edmunds building back then was like landing on the moon, and today's students just made a big move of their own, out of the downtown Macy's campus.

[State Board of Education Chair Resigns Abruptly](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/education-news/state-board-of-education-chair-resigns-abruptly)

❝

*"There’s nothing that happened. There’s nothing going on in my life, which, in a way, makes it all the more puzzling," per Seven Days.*

Jennifer Deck Samuelson, a Manchester lawyer on the State Board of Education since 2020 and its chair for four years, resigned in a September 15 letter to Gov. Phil Scott, saying the year ahead will be "extra busy on all fronts" and pointing to work and family too. The board is building the rubric for judging school district merger proposals with merger committees expected to start meeting next month, and it also has to finalize statewide graduation requirements within the year. Vice chair Tammy Kolbe is acting chair until the October meeting, and the Senate's Committee on Committees will fill the open seat.

[Federal grants invigorate efforts to address veteran homelessness in Vermont](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/21/federal-grants-invigorate-efforts-to-address-veteran-homelessness-in-vermont)

❝

*"No veteran should be homeless in the country they fought to defend," per VTDigger.*

The VA awarded $18,499,223 to organizations across Vermont and New Hampshire that house and support veterans, part of $1.17 billion going to 680 groups nationwide. Vermont counted 111 unhoused veterans in 2025, 3% of the state's unhoused population and up from 107 the year before, and part of the money goes to two transitional housing programs in Northfield and Bradford, Veterans' Place and Veterans Inc.. The White River Junction VA helped 107 local veterans find homes in 2025, and it is encouraging landlords to rent to veterans in its programs.

[More low-income seniors in Vermont are now eligible for help paying for Medicare](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-09-23/more-low-income-seniors-in-vermont-are-now-eligible-for-help-paying-for-medicare)

❝

*"This is such a hard moment in terms of health access. There's so much bad news out there. This is a bright spot," per Vermont Public.*

Vermont raised the income limits for its Medicare Savings Program, which helps seniors pay Medicare premiums and sometimes co-pays and deductibles, and nearly 12,000 Vermonters have already enrolled. Carole Patten, 78, of Lake Elmore now keeps an extra $200 a month from her Social Security check, and there is no asset test, so a house or money in the bank doesn't count against you. You can sign up anytime through Green Mountain Care at 1-800-250-8427, no need to wait for open enrollment on October 15.

[A celebration of 'maple-fired' punk highlights challenging times for Vermont's live music](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/22/a-celebration-of-maple-fired-punk-highlights-challenging-times-for-vermonts-live-music)

❝

*"A lot of folks tell me that Burlington used to be like the main scene for punk bands," per VTDigger.*

Randolph's brand new Skunkfest was set for Saturday outside The Underground with nine punk and ska bands, a makers' market and a fixer tent, but owner Vincent Freeman says the venue's regular shows go on indefinite hiatus by the end of the year after his insurer dropped him and music licensing costs added up. Vallory Falls drummer Riley Rae Burridge, whose band calls itself maple fired punk rock, points to the Burlington rooms that are gone, with Radio Bean closing earlier this year and Jim's Basement and 242 Main both shut within the past 10 years. She also calls punk one of the most caring communities in the state, where she has felt very accepted as a trans person.

['Mend it and keep it alive': Inside Vermont's growing repair culture](https://vtcommunitynews.org/2026/09/22/mend-it-and-keep-it-alive-inside-vermonts-growing-repair-culture)

❝

*"My mom grew up on a farm. The first move was always to try to mend it and keep it alive," per Community News Service.*

Every third Saturday, volunteers at the Old North End Repair Cafe at 12-22 North Street fix one another's broken belongings over bagels, and it is one of the spots listed in ReuseVT, a new state funded directory of repair cafes, repair businesses and lending libraries. Director Susan Alexander says the hard part is economics, since a $27 toaster online can beat a $35 fix from the guy down the road, even though repair keeps local dollars local. Vermont generates about 912,180 tons of waste a year, and Alexander would love to see an annual gathering built entirely around reused and repaired goods.

[First Bite: The Bidwell Extends an All-Day Waterbury Welcome](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/food-drink/restaurantreviews/first-bite-the-bidwell-extends-an-all-day-waterbury-welcome)

❝

*"I always want people to come however they are and get whatever they’re looking for," per Seven Days.*

The Bidwell opened in June in the former Stowe Street Café space in Waterbury, and every dish and drink is available all day, from 9 a.m. to 9 p.m. Co-owner Rachael Brown, most recently of Prohibition Pig, wrote a wine list that describes bottles through TV characters from Schitt's Creek, Firefly and Gilmore Girls, while chef Tiffany Caldwell, formerly Heirloom Hospitality's commissary chef, turns out shareables like a golden beet sandwich ($13) and herby meatballs ($17). There is also a permanent Udder Guys ice cream counter in the front corner. Brown tells her friends from New York City that she lives in the real life Stars Hollow.

## UVM Athletics: Men's Soccer Blanks No. 25 Bryant as Basketball Is Picked to Win America East

No. 23 men's soccer [beat No. 25 Bryant 4-0 on Saturday night](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/9/26/mens-soccer-23-mens-soccer-cruises-to-4-0-victory-over-25-bryant-during-conference-opener.aspx) at Virtue Field to open America East play, what coach Adrian Dubois called "a total team performance from all 28 guys." At 7-1, the Cats open America East play Friday at 3 at New Hampshire.

Both teams are back at the New England Championships in Hopkinton, New Hampshire on Friday, October 9.
