## Local News (All Links Clickable)

[Burlington Technical Center opens permanent home at new high school](https://www.wcax.com/2026/08/28/burlington-technical-center-opens-permanent-home-new-high-school)

❝

*"It gives us more space, capacity; there is an increased demand in our region and across the state," per WCAX.*

Six years after PCB concerns closed the old building, the Burlington Technical Center's five programs are finally back under one roof at the new Burlington High School, with additional offerings at the airport. Students walked into classrooms ranging from a criminal justice suite with an interrogation room to a health science academy built around a real-world hospital environment. Director Jason Reed says the space lets the center meet growing regional demand for technical education.

[Ribbon cutting celebrates completion of Burlington Wildways Trail](https://www.mynbc5.com/article/burlington-wildways-trail-completed/73560446)

❝

*"It's a wondrous trail through the wild heart of Burlington. This is a great day and a great accomplishment for our small city," per NBC5.*

Nearly ten years in the making, the ten mile Wildways Trail is officially complete, linking the Intervale, Ethan Allen Homestead, Ethan Allen Park and Rock Point in one continuous route from the Winooski River to Lake Champlain. Saturday's ribbon cutting at the Homestead ended the only way it should have, with a guided hike, and the full route is now open to everyone.

[Burlington mayor meets with businesses over downtown disorder](https://www.wcax.com/2026/08/28/burlington-mayor-meets-with-businesses-over-downtown-disorder)

❝

*"We need to gather together to make sure this stops. I'm begging," per WCAX.*

A standing room only crowd packed City Hall on Friday as downtown businesses told Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak they are losing patience with open drug use, vagrancy and safety issues, with the Friendly Toast's manager describing a recent needle stick and Vermont Pub and Brewery reporting foot traffic down 20 percent from an already bad year. The mayor said she is not okay with the status quo and pointed to more police hiring, portable bathrooms and prevention work, while stressing the city cannot do it alone without state backing for a new 100-bed shelter. With Gov. Scott panning that idea days earlier, the standoff between the city and Montpelier is the thing to watch this fall.

[Why Vermont's housing shortage can't be explained by population size alone](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-08-25/vermonts-housing-shortage-cant-be-explained-population-size-alone)

❝

*"This is a beautiful screened-in porch, which I now never use. It's a lot of house for one person," per Vermont Public.*

If the population is flat, why is it still so hard to find a place to live? This deep dive lands on household size, with 70 percent of Vermont households now just one or two people and the state average down to 2.2, one of the smallest in the country. The same number of people simply takes up more homes, and the Vermont Housing Finance Agency calculates the state needed nearly 22,000 additional homes between 2015 and 2024 just to absorb that shrinkage. With nearly 60 percent of Vermont homes built with three or more bedrooms and downsizing options scarce and expensive, older owners stay put, the housing ladder jams, and younger families get squeezed at the bottom.

[Gov. Scott calls proposed 100-bed shelter in Chittenden County a bad idea](https://www.wcax.com/2026/08/26/gov-scott-calls-proposed-100-bed-shelter-chittenden-county-bad-idea)

❝

*"I think, myself, a 100-unit in one location is a bad idea, but having 100 available beds is a good idea," per WCAX.*

The governor doubled down on his criticism of the City Council's 10-point homelessness resolution, saying he was surprised to see the 100-bed low barrier shelter on the list and that he would rather see smaller shelters spread across the county. He also suggested Vermonters elsewhere might be miffed at Burlington asking for extra resources when other regions face the same problems. Paired with Friday's City Hall meeting above, the two sides are talking past each other while downtown feels the strain.

[UVM president says school's budget deficit is closed despite declining enrollment](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/25/uvm-president-says-schools-budget-deficit-is-closed-despite-declining-enrollment)

❝

*"We will not be daunted in the face of the challenges before us," per VTDigger.*

President Marlene Tromp used her first State of the University address to announce the projected $12 million deficit is fully closed, done through eliminating vacant positions and operational inefficiencies rather than layoffs. Enrollment is still the headwind, with about 2,600 first years against a usual target of 3,000 and roughly 10,800 students overall as the demographic cliff hits New England schools hardest. The bright spot was research, where UVM pulled in $222.6 million for fiscal 2026 despite federal cuts, and a major fundraising campaign is coming.

[49,000 Vermonters could lose Medicaid in the new year](https://www.mychamplainvalley.com/top-stories/49000-vermonters-could-lose-medicaid-in-the-new-year)

❝

*"Part of our big kind of education push, is to make sure we have the right information for those folks, because we want to minimize any sort of risk of losing insurance as we start to implement these big federal changes," per MyChamplainValley.*

Federal Medicaid changes hit in waves, with several hundred immigrants losing eligibility October 1 and up to 49,000 Vermonters at risk on January 1 when new work requirements take effect, requiring at least $580 in monthly income or 80 hours of work, school, training or community service. Reviews also go to every six months starting in 2027. The state has launched StayCoveredVT.com so people can check whether the changes affect them, worth passing along to anyone on Medicaid.

[Abuse claimants fight Vermont Catholic Church plan to sell Rice Memorial High to supporters](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/25/abuse-claimants-fight-vermont-catholic-church-plan-to-sell-rice-memorial-high-to-supporters)

❝

*"The proposed sale is a functional insider transaction," per VTDigger.*

Lawyers for more than 100 clergy abuse claimants are challenging the diocese's plan to sell Rice Memorial High School to a supporter group for $4.3 million, just 14 percent of the $30.5 million value in the church's own appraisal, noting the property was never listed, brokered or opened to competing offers. The creditors want a public sale to raise more money for abuse settlements, while the diocese argues an open process would scare off enrollment and sink the deal. A bankruptcy judge takes it up in Burlington on September 1.

[The Closures of Four Dairy Plants Lead to Worry in Vermont](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/food-drink/agriculture/the-closures-of-four-dairy-plants-lead-to-worry-in-vermont)

❝

*"The bottom line is, this plant is vital to dairy farmers; it is vital to this entire community. It must not be closed," per Seven Days.*

Four milk processing plants have closed or announced closures in a matter of months, in Barre, Georgia, Enosburg Falls and St. Albans, and the losses have farmers and state leaders worried about the $5.4 billion dairy sector's future. The St. Albans Creamery closure stings most, since the plant sold cream to Ben & Jerry's across town and its co-op owner had pledged $30 million in investment after its 2019 merger, and Sen. Bernie Sanders is publicly calling for it to reopen while the Teamsters arbitrate a retaliation claim. The backdrop is stark, with Vermont down from 838 dairy farms to 405 in a decade and milk production falling since 2016 as huge modern plants in New York soak up the region's supply. Even Ben & Jerry's says it may not manage a majority of Vermont dairy in its pints this year.

['I'll take money from anyone': Corporate cash fuels some Vermont political campaigns](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-08-27/corporate-cash-fuels-some-vermont-political-campaigns)

❝

*"For someone who decides not to take corporate donations, they're really handicapping themselves," per Vermont Public.*

A Vermont Public analysis found Gov. Scott has taken $167,700 from corporations, businesses and corporate PACs over three cycles, 22.7 percent of his donations, while Attorney General Charity Clark has accepted $84,000, including checks from gambling companies and firms her office regulates or has sued. Both defend the practice, with Scott shrugging that there is no quid pro quo and Clark arguing candidates who refuse corporate money handicap themselves, especially women. The issue is live this cycle because gubernatorial nominee Amanda Janoo has sworn off corporate donations entirely, and she and Molly Gray are proposing a Hawaii-style state ban. National polling suggests voters are skeptical of the no-influence argument, with 92 percent calling corruption a big problem in politics.

[Problem Properties: Burlington publishes list of noncompliant vacant buildings](https://www.wcax.com/2026/08/24/problem-properties-burlington-publishes-list-noncompliant-vacant-buildings)

❝

*"This is a way for us to let folks know we are not leaving that property. We're going to stay with it until it comes into compliance or it comes back into productive use," per WCAX.*

The city published a top ten list of noncompliant vacant buildings, naming properties like the old Bove's on Pearl Street, vacant since 2019 with violations that make it unsafe for emergency crews to enter, and a former gas station with no redevelopment plans. Owners on the list owe fees under the vacant building ordinance or have visible maintenance problems, and one landlord with two listed properties argues the public shaming just invites break-ins. City-owned Memorial Auditorium is exempt from fees but will be added when the list expands, so the accountability cuts both ways.

[As Trump tries to curtail mail-in voting, Vermont officials clean up errors on state voter rolls](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/25/as-trump-tries-to-curtail-mail-in-voting-vermont-officials-clean-up-errors-on-state-voter-rolls)

❝

*"Mail-in ballots work well in Vermont, and I remain committed to defending our system from federal overreach," per VTDigger.*

The state removed just under 900 people from the voter rolls who were wrongly registered by DMV clerical errors going back 25 years, none of whom ever cast a ballot, all of whom had actively declined registration. Gov. Scott and Secretary of State Sarah Copeland Hanzas announced the cleanup themselves, framing it as getting the rolls airtight before general election ballots mail out next month, one day after a Supreme Court ruling revived attention on the White House's push against mail-in voting. Both officials, a Republican and a Democrat, used the podium to defend Vermont's universal mail-ballot system.

[New rules for Vermont vehicle inspections begin next month](https://www.mynbc5.com/article/vt-vehicle-inspection-changes/73536489)

❝

*"The goal here was to reduce how many Vermonters are failing their annual vehicle safety inspection because of things that have nothing to do with keeping themselves or other drivers safe," per NBC5.*

Starting September 1, your car can no longer fail inspection for rotor rust, tire cuts, windshield cracks or ABS and airbag warning lights, though mechanics can still recommend the fixes. If a station fails you solely for one of those, the DMV Highway Patrol says report it and they will investigate.

[Apps entice young Vermonters to join the 'birding boom'](https://www.willistonobserver.com/living/living_green/apps-entice-young-vermonters-to-join-the-birding-boom/article_1706513c-2399-44ee-88cc-0dfe30368fd0.html)

❝

*"The app democratizes birding. You don't really need any kind of special training to know what birds are singing all around you. You can just walk around in the woods and have your Merlin app out," per Williston Observer.*

Free apps like Merlin Bird ID and eBird are pulling Gen Z into a hobby long associated with the 55 and up crowd, and the local numbers back it up, with UVM's ornithology class doubling to around 60 students a semester and campus Audubon chapters nationally growing from a dozen to over 92 since 2019. Young Burlington birders describe group chats that light up when a good bird shows at Colchester Pond or Shelburne Bay. A wholesome one to close on, and a free hobby that starts in your backyard.

## UVM Athletics: Field Hockey Off to a Flyer as Men's Soccer Draws a Ranked Thriller

Virtue Field was packed for a big one Friday night. [No. 7 Vermont and No. 18 Charlotte played to a 2-2 draw](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/8/28/mens-soccer-7-mens-soccer-and-18-charlotte-duel-ends-in-2-2-draw-in-front-of-a-sold-out-virtue-field.aspx) in front of a sold out crowd, with Pieter Bultman heading home in the 22nd minute, Shalom Adja scoring his first UVM goal in the 58th, and keeper Niklas Herceg tying a career high with 7 saves to keep the point. The men sit at 2-0-1 and are right back at it Tuesday night when Holy Cross visits for Pep Rally night.
