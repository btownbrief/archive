## Weather & Weekend Rundown:

Friday's soggy morning gives way to a gorgeous fall weekend. The rain tapers off this afternoon with a high in the mid 60s, and the evening dries out around 60. Saturday is sunny and crisp with a high near 59 and a light north wind, but it cools off fast after dark, down to around 41 overnight. Sunday is mostly sunny and a few degrees warmer with a high near 65 and a breezy south wind, and clouds move in Sunday night ahead of a cold front with a chance of showers Monday morning. Frost looks likely Tuesday and Wednesday mornings behind it, and the leaves in most of the high terrain are peaking right on time. Track it all week, with patio, swim, and sunset scores, at [Burlington Right Now](https://guide.btownbrief.com/weather.html).

Friday kicks off [UVM Weekend](https://events.uvm.edu/event/uvm-weekend-2026), three days of games, open houses and reunions all over campus that are free to register for. ## Local News (All Links Clickable)

[Bowl & Roll Opens in Burlington](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/food-drink/bowl-roll-opens-in-burlington)

❝

*"Edamame, seaweed salad and pan-seared dumplings share the appetizer menu with golden samosas, while house sauces reference butter chicken and tamarind," per Seven Days.*

Bowl & Roll opened Thursday at 200 St. Paul Street, the former Magic Chopsticks space, with poke bowls, fire grilled rice bowls, build your own bowls and kati roll style wraps, plus boba tea on the way. Chef Santosh Bhattarai moved from California to open it with his longtime friend Neeraj Tiwari, who has co-owned Namaste Indian-Nepali Kitchen in Montpelier since 2022. It's counter service with roughly 50 seats, open daily for lunch and dinner.

[Winooski multiyear revitalization project complete as Main Street officially reopens](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/30/winooski-multiyear-revitalization-project-complete-main-street-officially-reopens)

❝

*"It's the main artery through town. We're only 1.4 square miles through the whole city, so we needed to make sure that all the pieces were going to be done right and the way the community wanted it to," per WCAX.*

Winooski cut the ribbon Wednesday on nearly 4,000 feet of rebuilt Main Street, running from the railroad bridge near Maple Street out to the Colchester line, after more than two years of construction and detours. The $28.3 million project added a bike lane, wider sidewalks, more than 200 plants and trees, new traffic lights, upgraded sewer lines, and moved the utility lines underground. City Manager Elaine Wang says it was a decade in the making, and the finishing touches coming this fall won't affect traffic or businesses.

[2 UVM Medical Center unions vote to authorize strike](https://vtdigger.org/2026/10/02/2-uvm-medical-center-unions-vote-to-authorize-strike)

❝

*"Union representatives said that 82% of voting members cast ballots in favor of authorizing the strike," per VTDigger.*

The support staff and technical staff unions, 3,300 members who have been bargaining jointly since April, announced the result Friday, which gives union leadership the authority to call a strike and start the legal notification process before anyone takes to the picket line. They want more say over staffing levels while the hospital network goes through layoffs and restructuring under tight finances. The vote followed the hospital's "last, best and final" offer last week.

[Church Street Businesses Sound the Alarm Directly to Gov. Scott](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/church-street-businesses-sound-the-alarm-directly-to-gov-scott)

❝

*"As you are well aware, Burlington's downtown is essentially in a state of emergency," per Seven Days.*

The nine member Church Street Marketplace Commission sent Gov. Phil Scott a letter Wednesday with four requests. They want the state to help CVOEO find a home for a 100 bed homeless shelter, put state troopers back on regular downtown patrols, bring back the accountability court for repeat offenders, and plan a state mental health facility to replace the Waterbury hospital that closed 15 years ago. The letter makes an economic case, noting Chittenden County contributes about a quarter of the state's tax income, after Scott shrugged off nearly identical requests from the mayor and a bipartisan City Council resolution in August.

[Burlington Police lieutenant resigns following multiple misconduct investigations](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/29/burlington-police-lieutenant-resigns-following-multiple-misconduct-investigations)

❝

*"The string of investigations started in January when a fellow officer reported that Henry spent hours in the break room while on duty," per WCAX.*

Investigators found Lt. Michael Henry spent almost nine hours of one day in the break room, then approached the officer who reported him, and a third investigation found he'd been overpaid more than $50,000 in the last year. On July 15, State's Attorney Sarah George sent a Brady letter saying she would no longer call him as a witness or accept criminal cases from him, and he resigned in August after 15 years with the department. The mayor's office says the city's investigation ended when he resigned, which is why he was never fired.

[UVM awarded record $38M to create 'AI twin' of sick patients](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/30/uvm-awarded-record-38-million-to-create-ai-twin-of-sick-patients)

❝

*"I think we've just shown federal agencies and colleagues and partners that we're capable of working at a different scale," per VTDigger.*

UVM landed up to $38 million from the federal Advanced Research Projects Agency for Health, its largest research award ever, for trauma surgeon Dr. Gary An's work on an AI "twin" that mimics a critically ill patient so doctors can test treatments on the model first. A doctor would give the AI a blood sample and get back a treatment recommendation, with the goal of shorter ICU stays, though research VP Kirk Dombrowski says wide use could be seven to 10 years away. The award comes a week after UVM's $19 million grant for a national neuroscience observatory.

[Officials pick new possible spot for OD Prevention Center](https://www.mychamplainvalley.com/news/local-news/officials-pick-new-possible-spot-for-od-prevention-center/)

❝

*"There is a very real possibility that this location will not be a good fit for this use. That is the reason we are not discussing the actual location right now," per MyChamplainValley.*

Burlington and Vermonters for Criminal Justice Reform signed a letter of intent to buy a property for Vermont's first overdose prevention center, which keeps it off the market for 90 days, CEDO director Kara Alnasrawi told the City Council Monday. The city will only name the spot once it's sure about it, and officials promised a door knocking campaign with neighbors before anything is final. Council President Ben Traverse asked whether the location could be shared by the end of the year and wants more detail on funding, since the center could cost more than $1 million a year with almost certainly no federal money.

[Heated Abenaki event confrontation in Burlington leads to arrest](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/29/heated-abenaki-event-confrontation-burlington-leads-arrest)

❝

*"One of their members pushed me, pretty significant push, knocked me backwards, and threatened me verbally," per WCAX.*

Rep. Troy Headrick of Burlington and members of the Odanak First Nation from Quebec were hosting a private presentation on Abenaki heritage with UVM historians and students when members of the Abenaki Nation of Missisquoi showed up, and the Odanak council says one person was arrested and released. Odanak leaders, Headrick and some UVM researchers argue that members of Vermont's four state recognized tribes have no Abenaki ancestry, while former Sen. Vince Illuzzi, who shepherded recognition through the Statehouse, says the tribes demonstrated their descent. Headrick and others have asked the Vermont Commission on Native American Affairs to speak out against violence.

[In a surprise move, Vermont's Roman Catholic Diocese seeks to dismiss its 2-year-old bankruptcy case](https://vtdigger.org/2026/10/01/in-a-surprise-move-vermonts-roman-catholic-diocese-seeks-to-dismiss-its-2-year-old-bankruptcy-case)

❝

*"It is choosing to litigate every issue the hard way and then blaming the bill on everyone else," per VTDigger.*

Two years and $2 million in legal fees into its Chapter 11 case, the diocese asked the court Wednesday to dismiss it, after creditors including more than 100 abuse claimants turned down a $29.4 million settlement offer that would average $247,000 per claimant. Creditors are also suing for access to an estimated $500 million in parish assets the church put in trusts in 2006, and dismissal would send the abuse claims back into individual civil trials. Judge Heather Cooper set a hearing on the request for Dec. 8, and the proposed $4.3 million sale of Rice Memorial High School is still on the court calendar.

[Gov. Scott sends letter to president pushing for more refugees in Vermont](https://www.wcax.com/2026/10/02/gov-scott-sends-letter-president-urging-more-refugees-vermont)

❝

*"When we have the capacity to help more people fleeing war and persecution, while protecting our national security, I believe we have a moral obligation to do so," per WCAX.*

Gov. Phil Scott wrote to President Trump on Thursday asking the administration to increase and broaden refugee admissions for next year. Vermont is set to welcome about 125 refugees in the next fiscal year, but Scott says the state has the capacity for up to 300, and he made a similar request back in 2020.

[Poll shows majority of Vermonters disapprove of ICE enforcement actions](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-10-02/poll-shows-majority-vermonters-disapprove-ice-enforcement-actions)

❝

*"With these ICE efforts, I feel like the Vermont way could go by the wayside," per Vermont Public.*

In the new Vermont Public poll, 62% of respondents disapprove of how ICE is enforcing immigration laws, 54% strongly, while 25% strongly approve, and the split runs deep along party lines with 89% of Democrats strongly disapproving and 65% of Republicans strongly approving. It lands as protests at the ICE surveillance facility in Williston continue, with the attorney general's trespassing charges against a dozen activists followed this week by federal charges against five protesters. Vermont GOP chair Paul Dame doesn't expect it to hurt statewide and local Republicans, saying Vermont voters "love a reason to split their ticket."

## UVM Athletics: No. 12 Men's Soccer Keeps Rolling as Hockey Season Arrives

Men's soccer [closed out nonconference play with a 3-0 win over UMass](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/9/29/mens-soccer-12-mens-soccer-concludes-regular-season-non-conference-play-with-3-0-shutout-victory-over-umass.aspx) on Tuesday night at Virtue Field. Aleksei Carnier headed in a Luis Fischer cross 1:52 in, Arthur Caldas tapped one home at 6:29, both of them first goals as Catamounts, and Pieter Bultman added a header in the second half for his team leading fourth of the season. Niklas Herceg made five saves for his seventh clean sheet, which leads the country, and his 23rd career shutout puts him alone in third in program history. The Cats are 7-1-2 and climbed to [No. 12 in the United Soccer Coaches poll](https://unitedsoccercoaches.org/rankings/college-rankings/ncaa-di-men/), up from 23, and America East named [Stepan Fiker its Defensive Player of the Week and Herceg its Goalkeeper of the Week](https://americaeast.com/news/2026/9/28/aemsoc-week-6-awards.aspx). They host NJIT on Saturday at 4 at Virtue Field.
