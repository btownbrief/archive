## Weather & Monday Update:

If you like your September crisp, today is your day. Monday is mostly sunny with a high near 68, a northwest breeze in the teens, and a cool night down around 47, so grab a layer for anything after dark. Tuesday is partly sunny and climbs to about 71, with clouds building in the afternoon and a few showers possible late at night. Wednesday is the warm one, mid to upper 70s, and any morning showers are gone by breakfast. Thursday is the day to plan around, mostly cloudy with showers likely from the afternoon into the evening and a high near 75, before Friday clears out and settles in the low 70s. ## Local News (All Links Clickable)

[‘Pretty electric’: Hundreds fill downtown Burlington for pride parade](https://www.mynbc5.com/article/burlington-pride-2026/73710163)

❝

*"It's pretty electric to just see so many people just being themselves," per NBC5.*

Hundreds filled Church Street on Sunday for Burlington's annual Pride parade, the first one run by a new coalition after the Pride Center of Vermont stepped away from organizing the September event. Nonprofits, businesses, college groups, elected officials and candidates all marched, Saint Michael's students among them, and the Flynn carried giant floating sea creatures above the crowd, which director Jay Wahl compared to protecting a fragile reef. The Vermont Human Rights Commission was there too, reminding folks that state law still protects people from discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity.

[Switchback Brewing fully shifts non-alcoholic beer production to Vermont just in time](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2026/september/10/switchback-brewing-fully-shifts-non-alcoholic-beer-production-vermont-just)

❝

*"We're incredibly fortunate that we were already preparing to bring production of non-alcoholic beer to our own Vermont facility," per Vermont Business Magazine.*

Switchback says its non-alcoholic beer is now brewed entirely in Burlington, making it the only in-house NA producer in Vermont, and the timing was tight. The company had been making Switchback N.A. with a Canadian partner because the dealcoholization technology was only available there, and its last contract load crossed the border just ahead of a September 29 ban on importing Canadian made NA beer. That final shipment still got hit with the new 50% tariff, which founder Bill Cherry says works out to $10,000 less the employee owned brewery can put back into the community this year.

[Jury selected for Jason Eaton trial, with opening arguments due Monday](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/10/jury-selected-for-jason-eaton-trial-with-opening-arguments-due-monday)

❝

*"The case centers on Eaton’s mental condition at the time of the shootings, with his attorneys expected to raise an insanity defense," per VTDigger.*

Opening statements are today in the trial of Jason Eaton, 51, charged with 3 counts of attempted second degree murder in the November 2023 shooting of Palestinian students Hisham Awartani, Kinnan Abdalhamid and Tahseen Aliahmad, who were visiting Burlington over Thanksgiving. Twelve jurors and 4 alternates were seated Thursday after nearly a full day of questions, much of it on whether jurors could return a verdict of not guilty by reason of insanity, and a broken sprinkler head set off a fire alarm that emptied the courthouse for about 45 minutes right after the panel was picked. The trial is expected to run a week, prosecutors have not charged a hate crime, and Awartani remains paralyzed.

[Changes at Burlington’s City Hall Park Aimed at Disrupting Bad Behavior](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/changes-at-burlingtons-city-hall-park-aimed-at-disrupting-bad-behavior)

❝

*"We’re trying to be flexible and see what works, what we can elevate and what we can let go of," per Seven Days.*

The city's restarted hot spot program, now led by Fletcher Free Library director Mary Danko, is bringing a mobile 360 degree security camera, ambient music programmed by Burlington City Arts, two portable toilets, blocked off parking spots on College Street and coordinated sidewalk cleanups to City Hall Park. The camera is a LiveView unit without the AI features of the Flock cameras, the music idea comes from the Wild Center in the Adirondacks and Brian Eno's airport compositions, and Danko says the point is a calmer park, not pushing people out. Police arrested 11 people there over the past month on low level drug charges, and the council takes up the Burlington Forward resolution again tonight.

[Burlington merchants feel the pinch as tax revenue stalls](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/08/burlington-merchants-feel-pinch-tax-revenue-stalls)

❝

*"That midweek lunch just doesn’t exist the way it used to, so that’s pretty dramatic, and the midweek nighttime sales are not what they used to be," per WCAX.*

Burlington's meals tax revenue has been flat for six years and is down almost 6% from 2023 to 2025, while South Burlington is up 1.8% and Williston is up 15%. Economist Art Woolf and restaurant accountant Leo O'Reilly both point to lost midweek lunch and evening traffic, with O'Reilly saying a recent summer stretch with no downtown programming produced sales that looked like November. Mayor Mulvaney-Stanak calls the city's event calendar a deliberate strategy to counter that, and diners will keep paying the 2.5% gross receipts tax, since the council made the temporary increase permanent to fill the budget gap.

[Kismayo Kitchen Closes in BTV; Property Gets FEMA Buyout](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/food-drink/foodnews/kismayo-kitchen-closes-in-btv-property-gets-fema-buyout)

❝

*"Kismayo Kitchen is for Vermonters by Vermonters." per Seven Days.*

The Somali American restaurant at 505 Riverside Avenue has closed, and the little red building is headed for demolition, most likely next year, under a FEMA hazard mitigation buyout after a geotechnical assessment found the slope behind it unstable, with the lot becoming green space. Founder Ahmed Omar died unexpectedly in 2023 at 36, his widow Anisa Mohamed reopened the restaurant three months later, and a 2026 sale to another couple was never finalized, so she still owns the business. She now lives in Minnesota, has new operators lined up, and says they are looking for a new Burlington space to keep her husband's name on a door.

[Vermonters will vote on constitutional amendment enshrining labor protections in November](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/07/vermonters-will-vote-on-constitutional-amendment-enshrining-labor-protections-in-november)

❝

*"Vermont would be the seventh state to adopt an amendment enshrining the right to bargain," per VTDigger.*

Proposal 3 goes to voters on November 3 alongside Proposal 4, and it would write the right to organize and bargain collectively into the state constitution, protect union shop agreements, and make it hard for a future Legislature to roll labor protections back. Only Illinois has gone as far on the right to work question, and the measure cleared both chambers twice with no senator voting against it last year, backed by AFL-CIO president Larry Moquin and Rep. Conor Casey's one man Yes on 3 campaign. Opponents at the Right for Vermont Foundation and 15 House Republicans argue it takes away a worker's choice about joining or funding a union.

[Retired goats find new employment as therapy animals](https://www.charlottenewsvt.org/2026/09/10/retired-goats-find-new-employment-as-therapy-animals)

❝

*"Their eyes bulge ever so slightly out from their skulls. They are sort of lumpy. It’s an automatic rush of serotonin," per The Charlotte News.*

Lauren Gitlin has retired the 53 goat milking herd at Villa Villekulla Farm in Barnard and is turning the place into a nonprofit sanctuary offering animal assisted therapy, after years of finding it harder to justify commercial production for animals she had bonded with. She came to Vermont from Brooklyn in 2014 for the cheese, started the dairy in Tunbridge in 2019 with three pregnant goats from Lazy Lady Farm, and now hopes to partner with a licensed mental health provider, with Moonrise Therapeutics near Woodstock as a model. The sanctuary is not open to the public yet and runs on donations, but a volunteer and a former intern both told the paper the goats already do the job.

[Absent State Action, Vermont Towns Move to Block Data Centers](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/constructiondevelopment/absent-state-action-vermont-towns-move-to-block-data-centers)

❝

*"Thanks to the governor’s veto, it’s kind of every town for itself, and that just doesn’t seem right to us," per Seven Days.*

Morristown has become the third Vermont community to block data centers, passing a two year moratorium on any high impact use drawing more than 10 megawatts, 57,600 gallons of water a day, 10 acres of pavement or 70 decibels of noise, after Royalton's five year ban and South Burlington's August zoning change barring centers over 20,000 square feet. The towns are acting because Gov. Scott vetoed H.727, the statewide regulation bill, in May, calling Act 250 and the Public Utility Commission regulation enough and the odds of a Vermont data center slim to none. VPIRG and Democratic challenger Amanda Janoo say leaving selectboards to face tech firms alone is nonsense, and Houston based PowerTransitions has floated a data center among the possible uses for the closed Vermont Yankee site in Vernon.

[What qualifies as a seasonal home in Vermont? New tax raises questions](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/12/what-qualifies-seasonal-home-vermont-new-tax-raises-questions)

❝

*"It’s a good opportunity to use that history to ask ourselves, well, what’s changed? And what’s changed is that we stopped building," per WCAX.*

Vermont has nearly 50,000 homes counted as seasonally vacant, 14% of the housing stock, and this year's education law raises taxes on the ones fit for year round living that are not the owner's primary residence, starting in 2030. Housing commissioner Alex Farrell says seasonal homes are actually a smaller share of the stock than they were in 1990 and the real change is that the state stopped building. Camps without winter water, sewer or insulation stay in the seasonal category and are not the target.

[Venetian Soda Lounge in Burlington’s Soda Plant Is for Sale](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/food-drink/foodnews/venetian-soda-lounge-in-burlingtons-soda-plant-is-for-sale)

❝

*"I just think Venetian really deserves an owner that can be present," per Seven Days.*

Justin Bunnell moved to Orlando in late August for his wife's job and is selling both the Venetian Cocktail & Soda Lounge and the Venetian Beverages brand, with the lounge for sale by word of mouth and staying open in the meantime. The brand started in the same Soda Plant building in 1917 with his great great grandfather's ginger ale, Bunnell revived it in 2017 and opened the lounge in 2022, and he says every performer is paid, with the business putting $30,000 to $50,000 a year into its jazz, blues, folk, trivia and karaoke calendar.

[Vermont Almanac, an annual compendium of Vermont life, is shutting down](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/11/vermont-almanac-an-annual-compendium-of-vermont-life-is-shutting-down)

❝

*"It was like a very cozy, by-the-woodstove book to read," per VTDigger.*

After six editions, the Vermont Almanac is done, its founders announcing in a newsletter that they will not open preorders for a seventh. Dave Mance, Virginia Barlow, Amy Peberdy and Patrick White, all Northern Woodlands alumni, started it on a $15,000 family loan, sold roughly 30,000 to 40,000 books and worked with hundreds of Vermont contributors on essays, rural how tos and natural history, and Mance says it is simply hard to publish a book in the 21st century when he also has to make maple syrup.
