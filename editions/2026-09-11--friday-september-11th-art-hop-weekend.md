## Weather & Weekend Rundown:

Art Hop weekend gets the forecast it deserves. Friday is sunny and crisp with a high near 71 and no rain anywhere in the picture, and it slides into the mid 60s by 7 and the upper 50s by 10, so bring a layer for the Pine Street walk after dark. Saturday is the warm one, sunny near 77 with a gusty south breeze, and the only shower chance holds off until after 11 at night. Sunday turns humid with a high in the upper 70s and a 50 percent shot at afternoon showers and storms, but the rain chance stays low through 1, so the noon parade should finish dry before the sky gets busy. Track it all week, with patio, swim, and sunset scores, at [Burlington Right Now](https://guide.btownbrief.com/weather.html).

Friday is Art Hop night, and we’re walking it together. The [34th South End Art Hop](https://www.seaba.com/arthop) itself runs 5 to 10 across the South End, and Friday is by far the most spectacular as Pine Street closes to cars from Kilburn to Howard for the night, Local Motion runs a free bike valet at the Barge Canal at 377 Pine, and there is free parking and shuttle pickup at the Innovation Center at 128 Lakeside Ave. And if you want to go, and also wanted a crowd with you, our own [South End Art Hop with BTown crew [MEETUP]](https://www.meetup.com/burlington-social-activites-group/events/316455686/) meets at 6 outside the main entrance of the Soda Plant at 266 Pine Street, hangs there for a minute, then heads out at 6:15 to wander studios, galleries, pop ups and food trucks with no set route, and you can peel off whenever you like. Along Pine Street, [Milton Busker & The Grim Work](https://community.sevendaysvt.com/event/milton-busker-and-the-grim-work-45485998) play a free set at the Venetian Soda Lounge at 7 and the [Third Annual Rocketship Show](https://www.instagram.com/p/DdE3mc6KVAN/) fills Conant Metal and Light from 6 to 10 with Rusty and the Big Jump headlining. Up the street, [BCA Studios opens its doors](https://www.burlingtoncityarts.org/event/open-house-bca-studios) from 5 to 8 at 405 Pine for a self guided walk through the painting, print, clay and metals studios, [Upstate Elevator's Lot Jam](https://www.helloburlingtonvt.com/event/art-hop-lot-jam%3a-free-concert-with-dobbs-dead-at-upstate-elevator/7937/) turns its lot at 699 Pine into a free block party from 5 to 10 with Clean Fill at 5:30 and Dobbs' Dead at 7, [three bands play the Green Door Studio](https://community.sevendaysvt.com/event/art-hop-at-green-door-with-radio-cowboy-danny-and-the-parts-dad-45485991) on Howard Street for free starting at 5, and Dana Mashoian Walrath's installation [The Book of Genocides](https://community.sevendaysvt.com/event/remember-repair-resist-the-book-of-genocides-45441254) is on view at the Maltex Building from 5 to 9. Downtown, [Justin LaPoint](https://community.sevendaysvt.com/event/justin-lapoint-45485964) plays a free 4 PM set at Hotel Vermont, the [Lane Series opens with jazz singer Cyrille Aimée ($5-40)](https://community.sevendaysvt.com/event/lane-series-cyrille-aimee-45327075) at the UVM Recital Hall at 7:30, comedian [Matt Mathews ($46.25-212)](https://www.flynnvt.org/see-and-do/matt-mathews/) plays the Flynn at 7 and again at 10:15, [All Your Friends](https://highergroundmusic.com/events/all-your-friends/), an indie sleaze dance party, opens its doors at Higher Ground at 8:30 for the 18 plus crowd, and Dennis McSorley's solo show [Typhoon of Tenderness](https://community.sevendaysvt.com/event/typhoon-of-tenderness-45464505) runs at Off Center at 7:30 by donation, Friday and Saturday. In South Burlington, [Rhythm & Reels](https://www.southburlingtonvt.gov/712/Rhythm-and-Reels-Sponsored-by-DR-Power) takes over Rick Marcotte Central School from 6 to 9:30, free, with The Night Shift playing, Yong Suk's Korean Kitchen serving and the movie Goat outdoors after the music, and up on Northgate Road, [AJH Dance Aerobics & Strength](https://frontporchforum.com/calendar/events/513912) opens with a free class at Northgate Community Hall from 5 to 6.

## Local News (All Links Clickable)

[Final preparations under way for Burlington Art Hop](https://www.mynbc5.com/article/artists-seaba-make-final-preparations-for-34th-art-hop/73674650)

❝

*"This is our Super Bowl," per NBC5.*

The 34th South End Art Hop opens tonight and runs through Sunday across more than 125 locations along roughly a mile and a half of Pine Street and the roads around it, and artists told NBC5 they have spent months getting ready. SEABA is running shuttles and bike parking, Burlington City Arts has music, visual projects and a kids take and make going all weekend, and Pine Street between Kilburn and Howard closes to cars from 5 to 10 tonight. Free to attend, with Friday night being the one to absolutely check out.

[Historic dams removed in Smugglers' Notch will reconnect trout habitat](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-09-11/historic-dams-removed-smugglers-notch-trout-habitat)

❝

*"This is going to reconnect the river for fish passage and water quality and flood resilience," per Vermont Public.*

Two derelict dams on the Brewster River in Jeffersonville are coming out, the first this week and the second in about two weeks, in a Smugglers' Notch Resort project with the Vermont Natural Resources Council that reconnects roughly 10 miles of trout habitat. The older one, Morse's Mill Dam, is a 50 foot timber crib structure from the late 1800s, and the Burlington connection is direct, since the mill it powered supplied a lot of the lumber this city was built with.

[Is Any Town Willing to Host a 100-Bed Homeless Shelter?](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/housingcrisis/is-any-town-willing-to-host-a-100-bed-homeless-shelter)

❝

*"We cannot successfully site and operate a shelter without partnership from local leadership and a common purpose around addressing homelessness," per Seven Days.*

CVOEO pulled its proposal for a 100 bed low barrier shelter at the former mattress store at 1717 Williston Road six days before South Burlington's September 8 council meeting, after city staff objected on strained services, crime and transportation grounds. CVOEO and Champlain Housing Trust say they have toured at least a dozen sites across Burlington, Colchester, South Burlington and Shelburne, the state has set aside $83 million this year for shelters, and Burlington alone spent more than $6 million on homelessness in 2024. At least 4,000 Vermonters were homeless as of May, with about 300 unsheltered in Chittenden County, and every town quoted says it supports the idea somewhere else.

[The New SEABA Center Is a Hub for Visual and Performing Arts](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/guides/arthopguide/the-new-seaba-center-is-a-hub-for-visual-and-performing-arts)

❝

*"People are elated to be back in that room," per Seven Days.*

Seven Days' Art Hop guide profiles the SEABA Center, the 7,000 square foot space in the former ArtsRiot building at 400 Pine that the South End Arts + Business Association moved into last fall. It now runs a free admission gallery, porch music, artist markets and a rentable performance hall, and it has taken more than 150 rental inquiries since opening, which tells you how much the South End missed having that room. STRUT runs there Saturday and the Designers and Wearables Market runs there Sunday, so you will probably walk through it this weekend anyway.

['An unprecedented day': How Vermonters learned of 9/11 — and who remembers 25 years later](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/06/an-unprecedented-day-how-vermonters-learned-of-9-11-and-who-remembers-25-years-later)

❝

*"We have to acknowledge that this is an unprecedented day," per VTDigger.*

Today is the 25th anniversary, and Kevin O'Connor's first person column for VTDigger tells it from the Vermont side, through Rutland native Michael Rosen watching from a block and a half away, former Port Authority officer Norma Hardy, who later became Vermont's first Black female police chief, and Bishop Kenneth Angell, whose brother David died on the first plane. At least 15 of the nearly 3,000 people killed had Vermont ties, and the quote above is Bernie Sanders, then the state's lone congressman, speaking to the Rutland Herald that morning. Burlington historian Garrett Graff turns up too, in the new Netflix documentary on the generation that grew up after it.

[Expecting Trouble: Vermonters Are Losing Local Birth Centers](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/gen-zero/expecting-trouble-vermonters-are-losing-local-birth-centers)

❝

*"It’s only a matter of time until a baby is born on the side of the road because someone couldn’t get there in time," per Seven Days.*

Seven Days' long feature on maternity care traces how Springfield closed its labor and delivery unit in 2019, Copley closed in 2025, and nearly one in three Vermonters giving birth now lives outside the recommended 30 minute drive to hospital obstetric care. UVM Health's three Vermont hospitals with labor and delivery lost a combined $4 million on it last year, with UVM Medical Center itself roughly breaking even. Families interviewed describe hour long drives in labor and one couple questioning whether to stay in Vermont at all. The piece went to press with Brattleboro Memorial planning to close its birth center in December, and the next story is what happened a day later.

[In reversal, Brattleboro Memorial Hospital will keep its birth center open](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-09-10/in-reversal-brattleboro-memorial-hospital-will-keep-its-birth-center-open)

❝

*"The Board believes strongly that access to local maternity care is essential to the health and well-being of our community," per Vermont Public.*

Brattleboro Memorial announced Thursday evening it will keep its birth center open after all, reversing a July decision to close it on December 31. The hospital credits funding approved September 8 through the state's federally funded Rural Health Transformation Program, $325,000 in pledged community donations, and an efficiency plan worked out with its obstetrics staff, and it says the final grant amount is still being settled.

[Vermont regulators finalize 5.5% rate increase for Green Mountain Power customers](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/08/vermont-regulators-finalize-5-5-percent-rate-increase-for-green-mountain-power-customers)

❝

*"I cannot afford taxes, utilities, insurance and food anymore," per VTDigger.*

The Public Utility Commission approved a 5.5% increase for Green Mountain Power's more than 275,000 Vermont customers, down from the 7.5% the utility asked for, and it takes effect October 1. Regulators approved about $113 million of the $341 million in four year resilience spending GMP requested, and the utility says storms are the reason it needs the money. The quote above came from a written public comment to the commission, and the backdrop is a state estimate from last fall that electric bills would climb 25% by 2030.

[Vermont Creemee Supply Disrupted by Immigration Enforcement](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/food-drink/foodnews/vermont-creemee-supply-disrupted-by-immigration-enforcement)

❝

*"Our farm is experiencing a severe labor shortage due to the detention of employees," per Seven Days.*

Kingdom Creamery of Vermont, the largest supplier of Vermont made creemee mix, cut production to a limited basis after Border Patrol detained three of its migrant workers in late July, all three were deported, and more employees left out of fear. Stands from Bristol to Montpelier have been scrambling for substitute mix from Miller Farm in Vernon or HP Hood in Massachusetts, and Holy Halvah in Bristol drove more than 60 miles for 80 gallons priced 75 percent higher without raising its own prices.

[Vermont awards first batch of $195 million in federal rural health funds](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/10/vermont-awards-first-batch-of-195-million-in-federal-rural-health-funds)

❝

*"It’s a shiny object meant to distract us from the impending cuts coming. That’s what we should be paying attention to," per VTDigger.*

Vermont announced the first $11.7 million of its $195 million in federal Rural Health Transformation money, with $9 million going to the Vermont Student Assistance Corporation for tuition benefits for healthcare students who commit to five years working in state and $2.7 million to three nursing homes and four teaching institutions for licensed nursing assistant training. The state has to allocate the full $195 million by the end of the year, and around 600 students are already getting VSAC tuition help under the program. The quote above is Rep. Alyssa Black of Essex, who chairs the House health committee and argues the one time money is a distraction from federal cuts still to come. This is the same program Brattleboro Memorial says let it keep its birth center open.

[Federal judge blocks Vermont AG's probe of 'Planet Hank' AI video](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/09/federal-judge-blocks-vermont-ags-probe-of-planet-hank-ai-video)

❝

*"This preliminary injunction is an important victory for our fundamental right to make fun of politicians in this country," per VTDigger.*

Senior U.S. District Judge William K. Sessions III issued a preliminary injunction barring Attorney General Charity Clark's office from enforcing a civil investigative demand against Hank Poitras over an AI generated satirical video of Rep. Becca Balint and candidate Mark Coester, the first real court test of Act 75, Vermont's new law on deceptive AI election media. The judge found the video ridiculous on its face and said the state's interest in protecting elections is lower when the threat is an obviously unrealistic satire. The injunction is narrow, covering only the June 7 video and the demand that followed it, and Act 75 itself stands.

[Ray's Is Closing Its Essex Junction Market. It's Also the Hub of a Fish Trade Almost No Other State Allows](https://www.compassvermont.com/p/rays-is-closing-its-essex-junction)

❝

*"By the accounting in MeatEater’s 2022 report, a Vermont perch changes hands about four times between the hole in the ice and the plate," per Compass Vermont.*

We ran the closing news last month, and the last day is now here. Ray's Seafood Market & Restaurant closes its Pine Crest Drive location in Essex Junction for good on Saturday at 7 PM after 74 years, so this weekend is the last chance for a lap. Compass Vermont's piece from August explains the part most customers never knew, which is that Ray's second site on North Street in Burlington is the hub of Vermont's unusual legal trade in angler caught panfish, a market the state narrowed on January 1 by banning crappie sales. The closing letter covers only Essex Junction and says nothing about the Burlington fish counter, which is still an open question.

[Album Review: Lily Seabird, 'Lightspheres on Their Way'](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/music/albums/album-review-lily-seabird-lightspheres-on-their-way)

❝

*"Seabird can devastate or charm you in just about any sonic setting," per Seven Days.*

Seven Days reviews Burlington songwriter Lily Seabird's new album Lightspheres on Their Way, out last week on Lame-O Records, and hears a turn toward louder, Crazy Horse style distortion after 2021's Beside Myself and last year's Trash Mountain. The record is only partly on streaming, so the reviewer's advice is to buy a copy at a local record store, and she plays Foam Brewers on November 15.

## UVM Athletics: Men's Soccer Stays Unbeaten With a Scoreless Draw in Boston
