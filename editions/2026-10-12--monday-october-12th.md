## Weather & Monday Update:

If anything outdoors is on your list this week, Monday and Thursday are the days to do it. Monday stays mostly dry and gray once the early showers wrap up, with a high around 65 and maybe a stray shower this evening before rain moves in overnight. Tuesday is the washout, with steady rain, south wind gusting to 30 and a chance of afternoon thunderstorms, adding up to around an inch by Wednesday morning while highs stay in the 50s. Wednesday opens with a leftover shower and stays cloudy and cool. Thursday brings the sun back, dry and chilly with a north breeze and a high near 55. Another round of rain arrives Friday, and the weekend brings the coldest air of the season so far, with Saturday stuck in the mid 40s and a few snowflakes possibly mixing in. Track it all week, with patio, swim, and sunset scores, at [Burlington Right Now](https://guide.btownbrief.com/weather.html).

[Sugar House Hotel in Winooski to Open Restaurant and Café-Bar](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/food-drink/foodnews/sugar-house-hotel-in-winooski-to-open-restaurant-and-cafe-bar)

❝

*"Creemees are among many Vermont touches at the new hotel, which boasts light fixtures by Burlington’s AO Glass, locally crafted woodwork, rooms named for real sugarhouses around the state and the Nedde family’s own syrup in bottles behind the registration desk," per Seven Days.*

The 115 room Sugar House Hotel opens Oct. 21 at 111 Winooski Falls Way, and both of its places to eat and drink are open to the public. In the lobby, Moss Café + Bar does espresso, pastries and grab and go sandwiches and salads by day, then wine, beer and cocktails with small bites at night, while the 75 seat Treetops restaurant on the eighth floor serves breakfast and dinner, with 40 outdoor seats looking out over the 100 acre nature preserve along the Winooski River. Food and beverage director Jacob Ennis came over from Warren's Pitcher Inn and hired executive chef Steve Zeno, formerly of Montpelier's Capitol Plaza Hotel, and sous chef Sean Richards, who owned Burlington's Queen City Café. The menus lean classic and local, from maple granola with Larson Farm yogurt at breakfast to a cider brined pork chop at dinner, and guests get a complimentary maple creemee when they arrive.

[Construction begins on new affordable housing in Burlington](https://www.wcax.com/2026/10/09/construction-begins-new-affordable-housing-burlington)

❝

*"This is a wealth-building tool for generations, not just for the first family, but for every family after," per WCAX.*

Champlain Housing Trust broke ground Friday on the Shale Beach Condominiums, 30 permanently affordable homes in the Cambrian Rise neighborhood of the New North End, with the two and three bedroom units expected to sell for $175,000 to $210,000. Under the trust's shared equity model, buyers purchase the home while the trust keeps the land, and when they sell they split the appreciation with the trust so the home stays affordable for the next buyer. The condos sit next to 100 Cambrian Way, another permanently affordable building that welcomes its first residents next month.

[Vermont governor’s race will test voters’ appetite for raising new taxes — or cutting them](https://vtdigger.org/2026/10/07/vermont-governors-race-will-test-voters-appetite-for-raising-new-taxes-or-cutting-them)

❝

*"I think there is consensus that affordability is a problem. And healthcare is a major part of that. But the approach of how to get out of that is unclear," per VTDigger.*

Democratic nominee Amanda Janoo, an economist from Burlington, wants a 2% surcharge on personal income over $250,000 and an 8% surcharge on income over $500,000, which Fair Share for Vermont estimates would raise $344 million a year, with the money going toward free primary and preventive care for every Vermonter. She also wants higher taxes on luxury second homes, commercial vacation rentals and corporate owned housing, plus a tax on big tech companies' digital ad revenue. Gov. Phil Scott, running for a sixth term, is pitching nearly the opposite, a still unspecified package of tax cuts or credits, and he has already told state agencies to plan budgets around less revenue. Scott argues a surtax would drive wealthy residents and businesses out of Vermont, though a Tax Policy Center fellow told VTDigger there is little evidence of significant "tax flight" elsewhere, and 57% of respondents in a recent Vermont Public poll backed a yearly tax on second homes.

[Vermont Legal Aid program to fight eviction may end due to lost funding](https://www.wcax.com/2026/10/08/vermont-legal-aid-program-fight-eviction-may-end-due-lost-funding)

❝

*"We have many elders who are housing insecure and are facing homelessness," per WCAX.*

Vermont Legal Aid's tenant representation pilot put a team of four attorneys on eviction cases starting in November 2024 and has opened and closed 207 cases, 54 of them for tenants over 60. It ends Oct. 31 because the state isn't replenishing the just over $1 million it got through Act 47, the Vermont Home Act of 2023. Legal Aid will keep offering free help, but project director Sara Kagle says less funding will make it harder to address Vermont's housing crisis.

[Despite some overall progress, Vermont test scores show a widening achievement gap](https://vtdigger.org/2026/10/05/despite-some-overall-progress-vermont-test-scores-show-a-widening-achievement-gap)

❝

*"Our current funding system is not equitable, and it’s resulting in a widening between the haves and have nots," per VTDigger.*

The state's first look at 2025-26 test results shows the proficiency gap between marginalized students and their peers now runs 30 to 38 percentage points, up from 26 to 36 last year, with the widest gap in sixth grade English language arts. The state counts students with disabilities, living in poverty, from racial or ethnic minorities and several other groups as marginalized, and they make up 57% of Vermont's roughly 80,000 students. There was some progress overall, as math proficiency in grades 3 through 9 rose 2.7 points and English rose 1.14, though only 41% of those students are proficient in math. Education Secretary Zoie Saunders says the gap makes the case for the foundation formula funding model the state is set to adopt in 2029, while advocates point to special education being siloed from general classrooms and to how schools respond to bias.

[GlobalFoundries brings massive solar array, battery storage online in Essex Junction](https://www.wcax.com/2026/10/05/globalfoundries-brings-massive-solar-array-battery-storage-online-essex-junction)

[Emerald ash borer has arrived in Charlotte, found at Mt. Philo](https://www.charlottenewsvt.org/2026/10/08/emerald-ash-borer-has-arrived-in-charlotte-found-at-mt-philo)

❝

*"Property owners should not remove healthy ash trees solely because emerald ash borer has been detected nearby," per The Charlotte News.*

The state's invasive species coordinator has confirmed the first emerald ash borer detection in Charlotte, at Mount Philo State Park. The beetle's larvae feed under ash bark and girdle the tree, and the most definitive sign is small, D shaped exit holes in the trunk, along with thinning leaves, vertical bark splits and extra woodpecker activity. Chemical treatment can be very effective on trees with 30% or less crown loss, so the advice is to inspect your ash trees, call a certified arborist if you're concerned, and buy firewood locally and burn it where you bought it.

[‘You can’t lose things like this’: Barre community rallies in attempt to save ice rink](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-10-08/you-cant-lose-things-like-this-barre-community-rallies-in-attempt-to-save-ice-rink)

❝

*"Once you shut down a building like this, it’s very, very hard to get it moving again," per Vermont Public.*

About a week after Barre announced that the piping in its 52 year old BOR Arena was full of leaks and the rink likely wouldn't open this winter, local tradespeople started volunteering to try a fix. Heating installer Ed Patterson, whose son is a senior on the Spaulding High School hockey team, came up with a plan to reinforce the failed system with plastic tubing, and almost a dozen volunteers are working on it with equipment the city agreed to pay for. A patched section held pressure in its first test. The arena still needs a new roof and humidity control, which could mean a multimillion dollar bond vote in a city that still doesn't have a school budget.

Burlington Parks wants to give the playgrounds at Baird, Ethan Allen and Smalley parks the same accessible equipment Pomeroy Park got when it reopened in July, at around $200,000 per park, with help from the Parks Foundation's Playgrounds for All Forever campaign. Smalley's wooden play structures were removed in 2020 after they began to erode. The in person feedback session at Smalley was last Thursday, and the city also has an online survey covering all three parks.

[Familiar faces from Norman Rockwell’s paintings visit Shelburne Museum](https://www.charlottenewsvt.org/2026/10/08/familiar-faces-from-norman-rockwells-paintings-visit-shelburne-museum)

❝

*"He believed deeply that using real people was essential for making his pictures feel authentic," per The Charlotte News.*

Norman Rockwell lived in Arlington for 14 years, creating more than 175 Saturday Evening Post covers there and famously paying his neighbors $5 and a cold Coca-Cola to model. Several of those models gathered at the Shelburne Museum on Sept. 26, including Mary Whalen, the grinning girl with the black eye in "The Young Lady with the Shiner," who remembered Rockwell getting down on his hands and knees to make her laugh. Don Trachte Jr. brought a list of the 211 people who modeled for Rockwell during his Arlington years. The museum's "Norman Rockwell: At Home in Vermont" exhibit is on view until Oct. 25, so there are two more weekends to catch it.

## UVM Athletics: McNabb Wins New Englands as Women's Hockey Sweeps Its First Road Trip

Zoey McNabb [won the women's 5k at the New England Championships](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/10/9/mens-cross-country-mcnabb-wins-and-vermont-men-collect-a-trophy-at-new-england-championships.aspx) in Hopkinton, N.H., on Friday, finishing 17.2 seconds ahead of the next runner. She's the first Vermont winner there since Judi St. Hilaire in 1980 and only the second Catamount ever named New England Champion. The men took third with 87 points, just two behind winner Providence, for the program's first men's team trophy position since it won the whole thing in 1958, led by Will Locke in fifth at 24:58.60. The women placed fourth, with Isabella Fryer running her fastest 5k of the fall. UVM hosts the [Fall Foliage Invite](https://uvmathletics.com/sports/mens-cross-country/schedule) at Hard'ack in St. Albans on Sunday, women at 12:30 and men at 1:15, its final tune up before the America East Championship.

No. 15 women's hockey swept Franklin Pierce to start 3-1-0, its [best start since 2022-23](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/10/10/womens-ice-hockey-15-womens-hockey-rolls-past-franklin-pierce-4-0-on-saturday.aspx). Friday's [2-1 win](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/10/9/womens-ice-hockey-15-womens-hockey-defeats-franklin-pierce-2-1-during-first-road-test-of-the-season.aspx) came on Zoe Charland's first career goal and a Lauren O'Hara goal with 45 seconds left in the second, with Ellie Simmons making 18 saves in her first start of the season. Saturday's 4-0 win had 11 different players record a point, including Gracesyn Louis scoring her first career NCAA goal in her first regular season game, and Tara Bach stopped all 19 shots for her second shutout of the season. The Cats play at Cornell on Friday at 6 and Saturday at 3 on ESPN+.

Men's hockey [dropped its opener 3-0 at Bentley](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/10/9/mens-ice-hockey-mens-hockey-battles-at-bentley-but-drops-opener.aspx) on Friday. Vermont outshot the Falcons 8-0 over the opening nine minutes, but Bentley scored on two of its first four shots, Colin Kessler hit the post in the second, and Lukas Swedin made 16 saves over the final two periods for his third career shutout. Eight Catamounts made their debuts, and Cédrick Guindon led all skaters with five shots on goal. No. 14 Boston University comes to Gutterson on Friday and Saturday at 7 on ESPN+ and WVMT, and Friday is the TD Home Opener, with cobranded T-shirts for the first 500 fans.

Field hockey (10-2) has won three straight, all shutouts. Consu de Castro deflected in a Veerle Nijhuis shot with 11 minutes left in the third quarter to [beat UMass Lowell 1-0](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/10/9/field-hockey-earns-first-conference-win-defeats-umass-lowell-1-0.aspx) on Friday for Vermont's first conference win, and on Sunday Emily Enden scored off a penalty corner 1:43 into overtime to [beat Appalachian State 1-0](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/10/11/field-hockey-wins-third-straight-match-defeats-appalachian-state-1-0-in-overtime.aspx), her first career game winner. That's double digit wins for the fifth time in six seasons. The Cats play at UAlbany on Friday, then host Yale on Sunday at noon for Rally Against Cancer presented by Mascoma Bank.

No. 16 men's soccer [won 1-0 at UMBC](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/10/10/mens-soccer-16-mens-soccer-scores-go-ahead-goal-in-87th-minute-defeats-umbc-on-the-road-1-0.aspx) on Saturday when Luis Vetter headed in an Aleksei Carnier cross in the 87th minute, its first win at UMBC since 2018. Niklas Herceg posted his eighth shutout of the season and 24th of his career, and the Cats (8-1-3) remain unbeaten in conference play. They host New Hampshire on Saturday at 1 at Virtue Field, with a free lavender beanie for fans for Rally Against Cancer. Women's soccer [played to a 0-0 draw at Bryant](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/10/11/womens-soccer-battles-to-0-0-stalemate-at-bryant.aspx) on Sunday, putting seven shots on goal to Bryant's zero as Sophia Kling kept her third clean sheet, and plays at NJIT on Sunday at 2. Men's basketball tips off with a [Holy Cross exhibition](https://uvmathletics.com/sports/mens-basketball/schedule) at Patrick Gym on Saturday at 4.
