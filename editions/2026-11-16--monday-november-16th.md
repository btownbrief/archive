## Weather & Monday Update:

Monday is the last gray one for a while, with clouds slow to break this morning before some sun sneaks out this afternoon and a high in the low 40s. Clear skies Monday night drop us to about freezing, so give the windshield a minute Tuesday morning. After that the week turns sunny, dry and mild, around 50 on Tuesday and the mid 50s on Wednesday and Thursday, with no rain or snow in the forecast through Thursday, and the Weather Service says it'll feel more like mid October than the week before Thanksgiving. Track it all week, with patio, swim, and sunset scores, at [Burlington Right Now](https://guide.btownbrief.com/weather.html).

[Stuck in Vermont: The First Musical at the New Burlington High School](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/arts-culture/stuckinvermont/stuck-in-vermont-the-first-musical-at-the-new-burlington-high-school)

❝

*"This month, the BHS Drama Club stages its first musical in the state-of-the-art auditorium," per Seven Days.*

After the pandemic, the PCB closure of the New North End campus and the windowless years in a former Macy's downtown, Burlington High School students moved into their new $204 million building in August. Now the drama club gets the new auditorium, and "Guys and Dolls" runs Nov. 19 to 21 under drama director Peter Bowley, a BHS alum who was in a 2013 "Stuck in Vermont" video himself as a student. Eva Sollberger's new episode tours the school and drops in on a rehearsal.

[Vermont National Guard soldiers return home after months overseas mission](https://www.mynbc5.com/article/vermont-national-guard-soldiers-return/74431746)

❝

*"Getting to this point and just knowing that when he steps off the plane, we have a new chapter of life ahead of us together," Alexandra Duffield said, per NBC5.*

About half of the Vermont National Guard's 86th Infantry Brigade Combat Team came home Saturday after nearly a year in Germany, where the soldiers were training members of Ukraine's armed forces, a mission the Vermont Guard took over in February. Families who spent months rearranging their schedules and taking on new responsibilities were waiting as they walked off the plane, and the rest of the unit was expected home Sunday. Welcome home!

[Teacher sues school district and newspaper to block report drop](https://www.vtcng.com/otherpapersbvt/news/local_news/teacher-sues-school-district-and-newspaper-to-block-report-drop/article_13ca6b36-c795-4db7-ac83-a66398a7aace.html)

❝

*"The public and the press has a right to know," Byrne said, per The Other Paper.*

South Burlington High School teacher Beth Adreon, co-president of the South Burlington Educators Association, has sued the school district and The Other Paper to block the release of former Attorney General William Sorrell's investigation into a so-called "black book" of negative commentary about district leadership that teachers allegedly circulated at a 2025 end of year party. The paper has requested the report since January, and once the union's labor charges were settled and the district told the union it planned to release it, Adreon's Vermont NEA lawyers went to court arguing it's an exempt personnel record that shouldn't exist because the investigation itself was illegal. Judge Megan Shafritz sealed most of the case but unsealed some documents after a Nov. 5 hearing, letting the paper report on it. Sorrell billed the district just under $17,000, and the paper's lawyer, Matthew Byrne, argues the public deserves to know what the district concluded about its own behavior.

[Vermont adds homeless shelter capacity, but it's still not enough to meet the need](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-11-13/vermont-adds-homeless-shelter-capacity-still-not-enough-meet-need)

❝

*"We just don’t have enough beds for folks to sleep in inside," Tom Morgan said, per Vermont Public.*

Vermont's community shelters had room for 721 households as of late October, up from 583 a year earlier, but January's count found nearly 3,300 people experiencing homelessness, so the state's extreme cold weather shelters will again fill the gap, now opening at 10 degrees instead of zero, with activations posted at GetWarmNowVT.org. Burlington's cold shelter, which typically sees 70 to 90 people when it opens, keeps its lower trigger of -10 windchill because that's when the city's emergency management plan activates, said Sarah Russell of the Champlain Valley Office of Economic Opportunity. CVOEO has tabled its planned year round 100 bed shelter after failing to find a site in time, and one possible location was nixed after South Burlington city leaders pushed back.

[Visiting the Boardroom, Burlington's Only Board Game Café](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/guides/kidsvt/visiting-the-boardroom-burlingtons-only-board-game-cafe)

❝

*"For $6 a person, you get all the gaming you can handle," per Seven Days.*

Kids VT took a family crew to the Boardroom, the two floor café and shop at 3 Mill St. on the Burlington side of the bridge to Winooski, where more than 650 games carry stickers rating them from "very easy" to "good luck" and a sign on the wall reads "NO WIFI. Play Boardgames. Talk to Each Other." The menu has 14 kinds of grilled cheese and a whole section of peanut butter sandwiches, plus fresh popcorn, a 10 by 10 inch pretzel with cheese dip and milkshakes topped with a Rice Krispies treat wedge. Owner Steve Joachim recently bought the café and is now its sole proprietor.

[Winooski commission votes against considering proposed horse carriage rides](https://www.wcax.com/2026/11/10/proposed-horse-carriage-rides-raise-concerns-among-winooski-residents)

❝

*"It’s a city park; it should serve city residents, not commercial businesses," Alissa Johnson said, per WCAX.*

Winooski's Safe, Healthy, Connected People Commission voted unanimously last Tuesday against the draft permit the new Sugar House Hotel would need to offer horse carriage rides in the city owned Casavant Nature Preserve, so the plan won't go to the City Council. Developer Doug Nedde pitched the rides, likely $10 to $15 a person, as a classic Vermont experience, while opponents cited animal welfare, the environment and safety, including horses and dogs reacting to each other. Johnson gathered about 200 signatures on Election Day to change the ordinance back, and 200 more would let the council adopt the change or put it before voters on Town Meeting Day.

[Population forecast highlights housing needs](https://www.vtcng.com/otherpapersbvt/news/local_news/population-forecast-highlights-housing-needs/article_a6716bda-c001-456b-beed-aa6a74832aa2.html)

❝

*"There’s some uncertainty. Nobody knows exactly what’s going to happen here in 2050," Charlie Baker said, per The Other Paper.*

Baker, director of the Chittenden County Regional Planning Commission, told the South Burlington City Council Nov. 2 that the city is expected to add almost 3,000 people over the next 25 years, more than any other municipality in the county, and may need almost 4,000 more homes just for that growth, while the state's housing target has it aiming for around 7,500 by 2050. Households keep shrinking, now averaging 2.09 people per home in South Burlington, as older people live alone and young people have fewer children. South Burlington added over 850 net homes in 2022 and 2023 combined, and Baker praised it as a leader on housing. The city may join the commission's housing needs assessment to learn what kind of housing it needs most.

[The Pinwheel to Stop Slinging Cowboy Cuisine in Essex](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/food-drink/foodnews/the-pinwheel-to-stop-slinging-cowboy-cuisine-in-essex)

❝

*"We just haven’t had enough staff to manage how busy we are," Wendy Piotrowski said, per Seven Days.*

Piotrowski and Andrew Machanic will close the Pinwheel, their Western influenced breakfast and lunch spot at 4 Carmichael St. in Essex, on Nov. 20, after running the Swingin' Pinwheel on Center Street in Burlington from 2014 to 2021 and then the Sweet Wheels Donuts bus. Thanksgiving catering orders are open until the closing date, so there's still time for one more Haystack Hashbrown or eggs Cassidy with corn cakes. The restaurant has been on the market since May for $225,000, and the couple say they're unlikely to open another.
