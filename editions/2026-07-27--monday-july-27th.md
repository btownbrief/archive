## Weather & Monday Update:

If you have been enjoying this dry stretch, soak up Monday, because it is the last clean day of it. We start bright and stay mostly sunny with a high right around 86, and the only catch is a few pop up showers and thunderstorms firing off in the afternoon and evening. Plenty of neighborhoods stay dry, though anything that does form can dump in a hurry, and the air turns noticeably stickier as the day goes on. Tuesday runs similarly with wetter results, dry through the morning and then scattered storms building after lunch. Wednesday is the soggy one, periods of rain with highs sliding back into the low to mid 70s and everything staying muggy. Thursday holds onto a few leftover showers before sunshine returns Friday. This rain is welcome after the dry run we had, so keep an umbrella in the bag and let the yard drink.

Monday itself is on the quiet side, and the anchor is [Kaleo ($49)](https://community.sevendaysvt.com/event/kaleo-vincent-lima-45337164) at Higher Ground Ballroom in South Burlington at 8, with Vincent Lima opening. [Doors open at 7](https://highergroundmusic.com/events/kaleo/), and the Icelandic band is out behind the ten year anniversary of A/B, the record that gave us "Way Down We Go." Baseball people get the cheapest night out in town at [Hot Dog Hysteria Night](https://www.helloburlingtonvt.com/event/vermont-lake-monsters-hot-dog-hysteria/7230/) at Centennial Field, first pitch 6:35, where the whole hook is 25 cent hot dogs, so come hungry and get there early. Runners can meet at [Skirack's Run Club](https://www.facebook.com/events/998796839426241/) on Main Street, which gathers at 5:45 in the shop's Run Center. Readers have the [Book Club For Curious Women [MEETUP]](https://www.meetup.com/vtladiessocialgroup/events/315459746/) at 6:30 in Essex Junction, which works through a couple chapters at a time rather than a whole book, under what the host cheerfully calls Vegas rules. Quiz types can settle in for [Trivia with Craig Mitchell](https://community.sevendaysvt.com/event/trivia-with-craig-mitchell-40943661) at the Monkey House in Winooski at 7. If you want something you can wander into any day this week, [Color & Scale](https://www.helloburlingtonvt.com/event/color-%26-scale%3a-leah-barranco/7738/) is up through September at the BCA Center, Leah Barranco's vivid paintings of the creatures living in Vermont's waterways. And at 2 in the afternoon South Burlington breaks ground on the [Walk Bike Bridge](https://frontporchforum.com/calendar/events/505224) near Staples, the crossing that will finally let people walk and bike over I-89 away from the Exit 14 ramps, with an opening penciled in for late 2028.

Tuesday fills up fast. Singers should know that the [Green Mountain Chorus guest night](https://frontporchforum.com/calendar/events/506971) at Grace United Methodist in Essex Junction from 7 to 8 is open to anybody curious about barbershop harmony, men and women both, and they are working up Beatles material. ## Local News (All Links Clickable)

**[Howard Center to Expand Services With New Federal Designation](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/howard-center-to-expand-services-with-new-federal-designation/)**

❝

*"This is the result of years of work by our team, and it changes what's possible for the people we serve with mental health and substance use treatment services," per Seven Days.*

Vermont's largest social services provider is now a Certified Community-Based Integrated Health Center, which unlocks higher Medicaid reimbursement and more federal funding, and Howard Center plans to add at least 20 direct care positions with it. The last year ran the other way, with 30 positions cut to close a $2.5 million hole, the Clarke Street needle exchange closed, and the six bed Jarrett House program for kids in crisis shut in June for lack of staff. Three other Vermont agencies earned the designation this year.

**[South Burlington plans Vermont's first PFAS-free synthetic turf field](https://www.wcax.com/2026/07/21/south-burlington-plans-vermonts-first-pfas-free-synthetic-turf-field/)**

❝

*"Synthetic fields are banned in Vermont because of PFAS contamination associated with the rubber infill and turf grass typically used. So, the city got creative," per WCAX.*

Rain regularly pools on the grass at Veterans Memorial Park for days and wipes out games, so the city wants a synthetic multisport field with a base that actually drains. The workaround on Vermont's ban is PFAS free grass plus infill made of small wood chip pellets instead of crumb rubber, which project manager Silken Kershner says runs cooler underfoot and plays less bouncy. It went before the Development Review Board last week and still needs Act 250 and stormwater permits, with play targeted for next summer.

**[Age Well's Day at the Champlain Valley Fair to bring older Vermonters together for a day of food, music and fun](https://www.colchestersun.com/things_to_do/age-well-s-day-at-the-champlain-valley-fair-to-bring-older-vermonters-together-for/article_c489eb87-1eba-4d69-8442-8f4aacba6193.html)**

❝

*"The University of Vermont Cancer Center has graciously come with their dermatology team to do on-site private skin cancer screenings," per the Colchester Sun.*

Age Well has run this for more than 15 years, and this year it lands at noon on Tuesday, September 1 at the Champlain Valley Exposition, with barbecue at 12:45, the Deuces Wild band and a raffle. An $8 suggested donation covers parking, fair admission, lunch and music, but only 350 tickets exist, six per person, and they have to be picked up in person at Age Well's Colchester office. If you have a parent or a neighbor who would love this, move now.

**[Vermont Towns Try Different Ways to Regulate Short-Term Rentals](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/housingcrisis/vermont-towns-try-different-ways-to-regulate-short-term-rentals/)**

❝

*"Despite the restrictions and added expenses, the number of Burlington short-term rentals has increased since the ordinance was passed, from about 200 in 2022 to about 300 now, according to city officials," per Seven Days.*

Burlington has some of the strictest vacation rental rules in Vermont, requiring most hosts to live on the property they rent out, with a 9 percent local tax on every booking on top of 12 percent in state taxes. That has raised $1.7 million since 2017 and nearly $300,000 last fiscal year, about a quarter of what the Housing Trust Fund takes in annually. Twelve Old North End owners sued four years ago and the case is still unresolved, so the permitting department has paused penalties while it plays out. Miro Weinberger, who vetoed a stricter first draft before signing the ordinance, now reads the fight as a symptom of Vermont failing to build enough, with vacation rentals under 2 percent of the city's housing stock while Stowe hits a quarter of its own.

**[United Way of Northwest Vermont awards $630,000 for substance misuse prevention](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2026/july/22/united-way-northwest-vermont-awards-630000-substance-misuse-prevention)**

❝

*"Continued funding matters because prevention takes time, trust, and sustained community effort," per Vermont Business Magazine.*

The $630,000 goes to 16 organizations as year two of a three year investment that will top $2 million, with United Way acting as lead prevention organization for the Burlington, St. Albans and Barre health districts. Locally that means money for the King Street Center, the BIPOC Youth Mentorship Program, the Richard Kemp Center, the Winooski Partnership for Prevention and the Burlington Partnership for a Healthy Community. Every grantee also got a 3 percent cost of living bump. A new request for proposals is coming in the next few months, which is the opening for groups not currently in the portfolio.

**[Vermont's Catholic Church aims to protect Rice Memorial High by selling the parochial school to supporters](https://vtdigger.org/2026/07/20/vermonts-catholic-church-aims-to-protect-rice-memorial-high-by-selling-the-parochial-school-to-supporters/)**

❝

*"The proposal is intended to preserve Rice's Catholic identity while providing a strong foundation for future generations," per VTDigger. "Students and families should expect no material changes to their Rice experience."*

The Roman Catholic Diocese of Vermont, in Chapter 11 since the fall of 2024, wants a judge's permission to sell the 400 student South Burlington campus to a new nonprofit called RMHS Inc. for $4.3 million, up from an initial $2 million offer. The point is to shield the 42 classroom building and its 27 acres from liquidation, since advisers told the court that absent Rice the property would likely be repurposed into housing. Judge Heather Cooper takes it up on August 28, four days before classes start, and the diocese wants a private sale on the argument that a public process would gut enrollment. Behind all of it, a creditors committee representing more than 100 abuse claimants is seeking access to an estimated $500 million in parish property.

**[Labor board approves Healthy Living union despite management's objections](https://www.vtcng.com/otherpapersbvt/news/business_news/labor-board-approves-healthy-living-union-despite-management-s-objections/article_04aa6e1d-652f-4d86-b8d3-c33a2b2427b1.html)**

❝

*"The employer's objections and offer of proof do not raise any material and substantial issues of fact that warrant a hearing," per the Other Paper.*

The National Labor Relations Board threw out all four of Healthy Living's objections without holding a hearing and certified Workers United at the South Burlington, Williston and Saratoga Springs stores, where employees voted 105 to 69 in April. Management argued the union spread misleading information and that organizers created an atmosphere of fear, and regional director Linda Leslie found none of it would justify overturning the result even if true. Organizing director Casey Moore says proposals are drafted and the bargaining committee wants a contract by Labor Day.

**[Burlington's Plan BTV New North End draws concerns over possible park development](https://www.wcax.com/2026/07/22/burlington-hold-public-meeting-new-north-end-housing-plan-wednesday/)**

❝

*"Selling a city park is like selling a national park; once it's gone, we're never ever gonna get it back," per WCAX.*

Plan BTV New North End comes out of 18 months of community engagement, and it hit a nerve at last week's meeting when maps and renderings appeared to show development near Starr Farm Park and Ethan Allen Park. City officials say there is no current plan for development in any city park, and that the renderings exist to test what people do and do not want in those spaces. How the next draft handles them will tell you a lot about how this process goes.

**[BETA Technologies Unveils MV250, Loganair adds BETA ALIA CTOL](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2026/july/22/beta-technologies-unveils-mv250-loganair-adds-beta-alia-ctol)**

❝

*"The MV250 has a reposition range of 1,300 nautical miles and carries a 2,000-pound payload within a tactical range of 250 nautical miles while delivering greater speed and higher operational availability," per Vermont Business Magazine.*

South Burlington's BETA used the Farnborough International Airshow to unveil the MV250, an autonomous hybrid electric aircraft aimed at military logistics, with a turbogenerator co developed with GE Aerospace. Kyle Clark's pitch is that building the defense platform on the same airframes, batteries and production lines as the civil ALIA keeps procurement and operating costs down. In the same week Loganair, the largest regional airline in the UK, signed a term sheet for five all electric ALIA CTOL aircraft with options for five more, expected in service in 2029, which would make it the first commercial airline in Europe flying an electric fleet. Loganair's chief executive says a March demonstration across Scotland showed potential to cut operating costs by up to 80 percent.

**[The current state of electric car charging in VT](https://www.willistonobserver.com/living/living_green/the-current-state-of-electric-car-charging-in-vt/article_956f89cf-a00c-4d7e-b65b-8aae4e53874a.html)**

❝

*"According to website DCFCTracker.com (DCFC stands for Direct Current Fast Charging), there have been 39 new or renovated fast charging stations installed in Vermont in the last two years," per the Williston Observer.*

Kevin Thorley of the Williston Energy Committee counts 114 fast charging plugs across those stations, all open to any EV regardless of manufacturer and spread well beyond the interstates into the Northeast Kingdom and southern Vermont. Fast chargers push 50 to 250 kilowatts, roughly 10 to 40 times what a home level 2 charger delivers, which gets most batteries to 70 or 80 percent in about 20 to 30 minutes. His worked example is Williston to Cape Cod in a Hyundai Ioniq 5, a 282 mile run needing one stop of under 10 minutes.

**[Vermont is not educating students with disabilities up to federal standards](https://vtdigger.org/2026/07/22/vermont-is-not-educating-students-with-disabilities-up-to-federal-standards/)**

❝

*"Vermont is not complying with the federal Individuals with Disabilities Education Act, according to a U.S. Department of Education assessment, and is at risk of losing millions in federal funding if it doesn't correct course," per VTDigger.*

The U.S. Department of Education rated Vermont as needing intervention under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act, a downgrade that puts the state alongside New York, Maine and New Mexico, and three straight years at that rating can cost up to $2.4 million annually. The assessment found Vermont schools meeting fewer than 60 percent of federal accountability measures, with a four year graduation rate of 67 percent for students on individualized education programs against 86 percent for everyone else. Vermont last met the standard in 2018, and the Agency of Education has gone without a state director of special education since January.

**[Study: Lesions on Lake Memphremagog Fish Are a Transmissible Form of Cancer](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/study-lesions-on-lake-memphramagog-fish-are-a-transmissible-form-of-cancer/)**

❝

*"The cancer cells behave more like parasites than conventional tumors, moving from fish to fish," per Seven Days.*

UVM researchers publishing in Nature have identified the black splotches anglers keep finding on brown bullhead catfish as melanoma the fish catch from one another, the first transmissible cancer ever documented in freshwater fish, and they stress it poses no known threat to humans. The tell was genetic, since DNA in cancer cells from different fish turned out to be more closely related to each other than to the fish carrying them. Co-leader Julie Dragon's team found no link to the Coventry Landfill, because the disease is spread evenly across the 32 mile lake instead of clustering at the landfill end.

## Vermont Green FC: National Final Bound! ⚽️

The men have officially booked their ticket to the USL League Two National Final for the second consecutive season! Battling through 95-degree South Texas heat and a late 8:00 PM local kickoff, the Boys in Green put on an absolute masterclass on Sunday night, routing host [Laredo Heat](https://vermontgreenfc.com/usl2-playoff-match-report-vermont-green-7-1-laredo-heat/) 7-1 in the National Semifinal. The dominant victory extends their club-record winning streak to 10 straight matches and pushes Head Coach Chris Taylor’s remarkable unbeaten streak to 41 games without a single loss as gaffer.

Nineteen-year-old winger Ryan Zellefrow had his best-ever performance in Green, scoring a brilliant hat trick, dishing out an assist, and winning a penalty. The attack was relentless from start to finish, with Efe Aror, Jeremy Francou, Andrea Di Blasio, and Angelo Ventrella all finding the back of the net, while Texas native Diego Rosas added another assist to his monster summer tally. With this massive win, Vermont Green is now just one victory away from becoming the first back-to-back USL2 National Champions since the Cape Cod Crusaders accomplished the feat back in 2002 and 2003; a historic squad that coincidentally featured Vermont Green Sporting Director Adam Pfeifer on the right wing!
