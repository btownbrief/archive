## Weather & Weekend Rundown:

Keep the warm layers handy for the mornings, because high pressure parks right over us all weekend with sunshine and no rain in the forecast through Thursday. Friday is sunny and cool with a high near 51 and a light north wind, falling to about 40 by 8 PM. Saturday gets a frosty start, around 31 in Burlington with most of the region waking up in the 20s, then warms to a sunny 55 with barely a breeze. Sunday is the best day of the bunch, sunny and 61 with a light south breeze, and right around 58 for the 2 PM parades. Monday stays near 60 with more clouds before a dry cold front brings a cooler Tuesday near 48, and the long dry stretch means worsening drought and a higher brush fire risk. Track it all week, with patio, swim, and sunset scores, at [Burlington Right Now](https://guide.btownbrief.com/weather.html).

## Local News (All Links Clickable)

**['Dispel the darkness': New art festival to shine this weekend in Burlington](https://www.mynbc5.com/article/night-watch-art-festival-burlington/74220991)**

❝

*"We're literally making it lighter. We're maybe bringing some hopefulness to a dark time in the sky and in the time of year, and in the country, frankly," per NBC5.*

Night Watch, a new outdoor festival from producer Pamela Polston and creative director Alex Reeves, brings light projections, installations and videos by more than a dozen artists from Vermont and Montreal to Lake Street between Main Street Landing and Foam Brewers on Saturday from 7 to 11, and it's free. It works like an art walk, and since many of the pieces have music or sound, you can even dance your way through.

**[Burlington elementary students learn to swim through first-of-its-kind program](https://www.wcax.com/2026/10/20/burlington-elementary-students-learn-swim-through-first-of-its-kind-program)**

❝

*"I didn’t need an adult today to swim and I’m not scared of deep water anymore," per WCAX.*

For the first time this year, Burlington fourth graders are taking swim lessons at the YMCA during school hours, twice a week for 90 minutes, in what school officials say is the first program of its kind in Vermont. All six elementary schools come for three weeks as part of the YMCA's nationwide Safety Around Water Program, learning water safety, survival skills and even how to save someone in need. The district wants to make the deep end swim test a graduation requirement eventually.

**[As Beta Technologies Grows, ‘Betaville’ Sprouts in South Burlington](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/constructiondevelopment/as-beta-technologies-grows-betaville-sprouts-in-south-burlington)**

❝

*"I can’t see how our property values are not going to diminish because of this massive project, particularly when Beta owns so many houses on our street," per Seven Days.*

Beta has spent about $22.6 million on at least 13 properties around its base at Burlington International Airport over the past year, and a master plan before South Burlington's Development Review Board would more than double its footprint there to 100 acres over the next decade. Fueled by the $1 billion it raised going public last November, the company plans to add about 1,000 local employees by the middle of next year, and it paid $8 million in 2025 for Pete's RV Center, a parcel appraised at $2.2 million in 2021. Shunpike Road resident Dani Fisette says a quarter of the homes on her street became transitional rental housing for Beta workers earlier this year, and Beta says it can't commit to long term leases there. The plan could be approved within the next few months.

**[5 takeaways from Phil Scott and Amanda Janoo’s first debate of the campaign](https://vtdigger.org/2026/10/22/5-takeaways-from-phil-scott-and-amanda-janoos-first-debate-of-the-campaign)**

❝

*"We can’t solve our problems with the same thinking that created them," per VTDigger.*

Gov. Phil Scott and his Democratic and Progressive challenger Amanda Janoo met Wednesday in a friendly first debate hosted by Vermont Public, where Scott leaned on his record through the pandemic and the 2023 and 2024 floods and promised to propose tax cuts or credits, while Janoo, an economist running her first campaign, pitched raising taxes on the wealthiest Vermonters to pay for free primary care. She cited modeling showing surcharges on income over $250,000 could raise $114 million to $344 million a year but didn't directly answer what she'd do if that wasn't enough, and Scott didn't directly say what his tax package would include. They also split on nuclear power, with Scott backing a small scale plant, and on remote work for state employees. WCAX hosts another debate on Wednesday, Oct. 28 at 7.

**[Electricity Costs Are Soaring in Vermont](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/topic/energy/electricity-costs-are-soaring-in-vermont)**

❝

*"Nothing goes down in price, and some go up exorbitantly," per Seven Days.*

Vermont's 17 utilities have asked regulators to raise rates 90 times since 2010, more than half of those in the past four years, and the average monthly residential bill has climbed 39% since 2019, from $97.25 to $135. Someone using the same amount of power as in 2019 now pays about $342 more a year, and the Department of Public Service predicts statewide rates will rise another 25% by 2030. Regulators approved a 5.5% Green Mountain Power increase in late August, below the 7.5% it asked for, and approved only $113 million of its $341 million in grid upgrades. Some places are seeing steeper jumps, with Hyde Park paying 35% more this year, Swanton seeking 30% after its Highgate Falls dam bladder tore and Jacksonville up 55% over three years.

❝

*"This was an honest mistake made by our town," per VTDigger.*

❝

*"We want to fix the problem of homelessness. We have the task of putting people into 700 rooms," per Vermont Public.*

Lawmakers and advocates want the Scott administration to reverse the Department for Children and Families' new motel rules under Act 143, this year's homelessness law, saying they delay or block people from getting shelter, and some are asking a legislative oversight committee to reject them.
