## Weather & Weekend Rundown:

If you have been waiting all week for the rain to let up, the weekend mostly delivers. Friday starts under patchy valley fog that burns off by late morning, then opens into afternoon sun with highs in the low 80s. Saturday is the warm one at 85, partly sunny through the morning before the clouds thicken up in the afternoon, with a few showers possible overnight. Sunday turns humid and unsettled because showers and thunderstorms fill in through the afternoon and evening with highs near 82.

[Full lineup & schedule for Burlington City Arts’ 18th Annual Festival of Fools](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2026/july/25/full-lineup-schedule-burlington-city-arts-18th-annual-festival-fools)

❝

*“This year’s lineup is one of our strongest yet, bringing together amazing street performers, incredible musicians, and some unforgettable surprises,” per Vermont Business Magazine.*

The 18th Festival of Fools takes over Church Street and City Hall Park today and tomorrow with more than 70 free performances, running noon to 9 today and 11 to 9 Saturday across four outdoor pitches with something starting roughly every half hour. Galumpha, Cate Great, Snap Boogie, The Yo-Yo Guy, Kilted Colin and The Pocket Circus are all on the bill, with Circus Smirkus Road Show and Jacob Green One Man Band added Saturday. Everything is free, tipping the performers is encouraged, and the information tent outside the BCA Center hands out daily schedules. So please, go on and enjoy Church St to the fullest this weekend! It tends to be one of the funniest, busiest times of the year.

[To the mayor’s surprise, Gov. Scott directs state police to patrol Burlington](https://vtdigger.org/2026/07/30/to-the-mayors-surprise-gov-scott-directs-state-police-to-patrol-burlington/)

❝

*“This is nothing more than the Governor wanting to make sure Burlington is safe for residents and visitors, despite others framing this as a political issue,” per VTDigger.*

Gov. Phil Scott directed state troopers to patrol downtown from July 29 through mid August, and Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak learned about it indirectly, then announced it herself in a Tuesday mass email saying the news had come to her attention the day before. His office pointed to a coming gathering of New England governors and Canadian premiers, an insurance industry conference, and UVM students returning for the fall. She said she welcomes state help while calling the lack of direct communication unfortunate, and the thing to watch is how troopers and the council’s own downtown plan land on the same blocks in August.

[Jordan Klepper and Steve Waltien On Comedy in the Trump Era](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/arts-culture/comedy-arts-culture/jordan-klepper-and-steve-waltien-on-comedy-in-the-trump-era/)

❝

*“I mean, I was at an event and an insurrection broke out. Don’t always see that coming. Sometimes things get strange,” per Seven Days.*

Klepper brings his solo show to the Flynn Main Stage tonight, a storytelling set built from a decade of interviewing Trump supporters at rallies, with clips and slides pulled from the road. Opening for him is Steve Waltien, a Shelburne native and Middlebury grad who wrote for “The Late Show With Stephen Colbert” until CBS cancelled it earlier this year, and who says he has never played the Flynn Main Stage despite growing up going to shows there. The two met in the early 2000s Chicago improv scene at ImprovOlympic and have been working together on and off ever since. Klepper’s read on the moment is that the rally circuit has quieted down and people are turning up mostly to laugh in a room together.

[First Bite: With Burl’s Downtown Kitchenette, a Diner Is a Diner Again](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/food-drink/firstbite/first-bite-with-burls-downtown-kitchenette-a-diner-is-a-diner-again/)

❝

*“It’s great to be able to say good morning,” per Seven Days.*

The Farmhouse Group spent three days in June turning the El Cortijo diner car on Bank Street into Burl’s Downtown Kitchenette, a nod to the space’s run as the Oasis Diner from 1954 to 2007. The 1953 Mountain View car was mostly left alone beyond new tables and booths, the existing staff stayed on, and the doors were closed only a few days. Executive chef Kevin Sprouse built a Southern leaning menu next to the diner standards, with grits at $4, smoked pork hash at $8.50 and a hot honey chicken biscuit at $15, and the pimento cheese is his mother’s recipe. It is the only one of the group’s five Chittenden County restaurants serving breakfast, it runs seven days a week now, and owner Jed Davis says mornings have been busier than expected while dinner is still finding its feet.

[Vermont establishes state’s first dedicated tourism brand](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2026/july/30/vermont-establishes-states-first-dedicated-tourism-brand)

❝

*“Vermont’s charm isn’t the problem, it’s that the brand story wasn’t giving people a clear reason to go,” per Vermont Business Magazine.*

Vermont rolled out its first dedicated tourism brand under the line “State of Inspiration,” built over nine months with the place branding firm DNCO. The research behind it is the interesting part, with 79 percent of American travelers calling Vermont appealing but only 15 percent naming it unprompted as somewhere they plan to go, and the state sitting 30th nationally for ad recall behind Maine and Massachusetts. Tourism is 9 percent of state GDP at $4.2 billion a year and brought 16 million visitors in 2024, so closing the gap between admiration and actual bookings is real money.

[Wild Meadows Books and Café Meeting Spot Opens in Williston](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/media-news/williston-newspaper-owners-open-bookstore-and-cafe-as-a-spot-to-congregate-and-celebrate-the-written-word/)

❝

*“We don’t see other independent local bookstores as competitors. Our competitor is Amazon,” per Seven Days.*

Susan and Rick Cote opened Wild Meadows in late June at 27 Market Street in the Finney Crossing development, pairing a full bookstore with a café pouring espresso, housemade lemonade, wine and beer, plus pastries from Mirabelles and truffles from Birnn Chocolates. The Cotes have owned the weekly Williston Observer since 2020 and run the paper out of a back room of the shop. They employ 20 people full and part time, stay open until 9 Thursday through Saturday, and have already hosted a Green Mountain Chamber Music Festival pop up concert. Until now the closest full selection bookshops were Hyperbole in Richmond and Phoenix in Essex.

[Burlington Dems Call for Crackdown on Drug Use, Encampments](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/burlington-dems-call-for-crackdown-on-drug-use-encampments/)

❝

*“There’s nothing compassionate or progressive about allowing our daily status quo to persist,” per Seven Days.*

Most of the council’s Democratic caucus gathered on Bank Street to announce Burlington Forward, a resolution headed to their August meeting that would expedite hiring for the 10 officers funded in the new budget and create public space response teams able to issue citations and connect people with services. It pairs faster removal of unauthorized encampments with more shelter capacity and some sanctioned sites, and asks the state and neighboring towns to carry more of the load. A sizable chunk deals with zoning that would put needle exchanges and the planned overdose prevention center through more public review, which is why the press conference happened outside Vermont CARES. Councilor Buddy Singh said 89 people account for more than 1,500 public safety incidents this year, and 40 percent of them have housing.

[No Excuses. Here’s What You Need to Know About the 2026 Vermont Primary.](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/guides/primaryvoterguide/have-you-voted-yet-our-vermont-primary-guide-can-help/)

❝

*“They’re not doctors, but Vermont’s justices of the peace do make house calls,” per Seven Days.*

The August 11 primary decides the Democratic nominees for governor, lieutenant governor and auditor, the Republican nominee for the U.S. House seat, and every one of the 30 Senate and 150 House seats. The part that trips people up is that primaries do not get universal mail in ballots the way general elections do, so you have to request an absentee ballot. Early voting runs through August 10 at your municipal clerk’s office, you can register right up through Election Day, polls are open 7 to 7, and homebound voters can call their clerk the day before to have two justices of the peace bring a ballot over.

[Winooski dog show crowns the ‘Best Pooch in the Noosk’](https://vtcommunitynews.org/2026/07/27/winooski-dog-show-crowns-the-best-pooch-in-the-noosk/)

❝

*“(The dog breaths) actually weren’t that bad this year. We have some good dog parents,” per Community News Service.*

Eighteen dogs lined up on the grass at the Winooski Farmers Market on Sunday for the market’s annual show, a $20 per entry fundraiser for the market and For the Love of Dogs Vermont, a rescue that rehomes around 800 dogs a year. Ahki, a three legged husky, took Cutest, Scar and his owner won Looks Most Like Their Owner on the strength of matching mohawks, and Penelope the Newfoundland won Worst Breath after drooling on the mayor. Judging that last category has been a mayoral duty since 2017.

[First-Time Candidates Richards, Janoo Vie to Take on Scott](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/politics/first-time-candidates-richards-janoo-vie-to-take-on-scott/)

❝

*“I’m not running against Aly, but for Vermont,” per Seven Days.*

Aly Richards and Amanda Janoo have never run for office before, met over coffee at the Williston Gardener’s Supply café in February, and agreed to keep the race positive, a pledge that has held up across a long run of shared forums. A July 21 UNH poll of 558 likely Democratic voters found 52 percent still undecided, with Janoo at 26 and Richards at 19, and put Scott’s approval at 48 percent, down 11 points from August 2024. Richards is running on regional housing targets, a temporary end to the sales tax on building materials and free community college paid for with cannabis revenue, while Janoo proposes a state funded preventative primary care system costing up to $320 million, covered by surcharges on income above $250,000. Richards has Kunin, Dean and Shumlin behind her, Janoo has the Progressive Party, the AFL-CIO and Jerry Greenfield, and with more than half the electorate undecided this one is genuinely open.

[GlobalFoundries signs $300 million award to accelerate U.S. silicon photonics leadership](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2026/july/29/globalfoundries-signs-300-million-award-accelerate-us-silicon-photonics)

❝

*“Silicon photonics is essential to AI infrastructure. For a decade, the industry talked about the shift from copper to optical as something that was coming,” per Vermont Business Magazine.*

GlobalFoundries signed a letter of intent with the Commerce Department for a $300 million CHIPS award to develop silicon photonics, the technology that moves data with light instead of electrical signals inside AI data centers. The work runs through GF’s existing operations in Malta, New York and Burlington, which is the local part, and targets next generation optical materials, wafer technologies and advanced packaging. A separate agreement hands Commerce an equity stake of roughly 1 percent of the company. The release also carries supporting statements from a run of customers including AMD, Broadcom, Cisco, Corning, Lumentum, Marvell, Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA and Qualcomm.

[South Burlington breaks ground on bike, ped bridge](https://www.vtcng.com/otherpapersbvt/news/municipal_matters/south-burlington-breaks-ground-on-bike-ped-bridge/article_0f18fd42-7a42-4164-98b4-44c6e127a468.html)

❝

*“It’s no coincidence that bridges are highly symbolic. They’re meant to convey, but more importantly, they are meant to connect,” per The Other Paper.*

South Burlington finally put shovels in the ground Monday on the bike and pedestrian bridge over I-89, more than five years after it was first proposed. The span will link Staples Plaza and Quarry Hill on the west side to the University Mall on the east, so you can get between the two cities without taking your life in your hands at an interstate ramp. Phase one opens to the public in 2028, federal grants now total $17.9 million against a price tag just over $27 million, and the bridge still does not have a name.
