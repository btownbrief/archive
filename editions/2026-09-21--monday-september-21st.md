## Weather & Monday Update:

Fall arrives this week and the forecast is playing along. Monday starts gray and cool, the clouds thin out through the afternoon, and the high lands right around 60 before a breezy evening slides into the low 50s. Tuesday is sunny from start to finish with a high near 64, and the equinox hits at 8:05 that evening, so autumn is official by bedtime. Wednesday and Thursday are the keepers, sunny to mostly sunny with light winds, highs in the upper 60s and nights in the mid to upper 40s, and there is no rain in sight until late Friday at the earliest. Track it all week, with patio, swim, and sunset scores, at [Burlington Right Now](https://guide.btownbrief.com/weather.html).

Language lovers have [Spanish Chat Mondays](https://frontporchforum.com/calendar/events/507135) at Three Needs from 7 to 8, beginners and native speakers both welcome, and [pickup dodgeball](https://frontporchforum.com/calendar/events/510535) runs at the Miller Center from 7 to 8:15, $5 a game with teams drawn at random. ## Local News (All Links Clickable)

[Fall foliage varies from year to year, for leaf-peepers and trees](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/16/fall-foliage-varies-from-year-to-year-for-leaf-peepers-and-trees)

❝

*"We haven’t seen anything even close to the level of damage we were seeing in the foliage last year," per VTDigger.*

Leaf season is looking healthy. Last year's drought browned trees across more than 64,000 acres, and this year the damage is minimal, according to Josh Halman, whose state team has tracked the same sugar maples since 1991. Their data shows the growing season stretching steadily, running 21 days longer last year than when the project began, though this fall's color may actually arrive a day or two early. Vermont's coldest zones peak first in late September, with each zone to the south following about a week later.

[New England Patriots Foundation Awards Burlington’s King Street Center $10,000](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/arts-culture/culture/new-england-patriots-foundation-awards-burlingtons-king-street-center-10000)

❝

*"We found the experience so rewarding that we signed up for the fall, winter, and spring sessions," per Seven Days.*

South Burlington's Cathy Seward was one of 26 people honored at Gillette Stadium last Tuesday with a Myra Kraft Community MVP Award, the only 2026 winner from Vermont, and the honor came with $10,000 for King Street Center. Seward has volunteered there since 2009, starting as a book buddy in the literacy program and then mentoring a child who has since graduated high school, and she is now on her second mentee. The 55 year old center serves more than 450 people a year, from 18 month olds to 18 year olds.

[Zachary’s Pizza adds a taproom](https://www.vtcng.com/otherpapersbvt/news/business_news/zachary-s-pizza-adds-a-taproom/article_3b9aa671-d124-4950-af7d-326b3b70e349.html)

❝

*"A lot of people just want to come in, have a drink or two, eat some food, and socialize, and it’s a good gathering spot for people," per The Other Paper.*

Zachary's Pizza on Williston Road in South Burlington has a new taproom pouring 16 beers plus wine, opened this summer in a former event space near the front of the restaurant after the arcade heavy family fun center in back closed in the spring. Owner Shawn Farrell says the fun center only made money about a third of the year, and the City Council approved a spirits license earlier this month so mixed drinks are on the way. The taproom sells pull tabs that benefit the South Burlington High School basketball program, Farrell is an alum, and it has added trivia and game watch nights.

[Solar parking canopy plan announced for Burlington Electric office](https://www.mychamplainvalley.com/news/local-news/vermont/solar-parking-canopy-plan-announced-for-burlington-electric-office)

❝

*"By installing a solar parking canopy at a City facility, we’re making smart use of land that already has been developed, reducing emissions, and continuing to model how we build a cleaner, more resilient energy future for Burlington," per MyChamplainValley.*

Burlington Electric plans to cover about 13 visitor parking spaces at its Pine Street office with a timber frame solar canopy, built with SunCommon and expected to come online in 2027. The bifacial panels should generate enough electricity for about 27 to 30 homes, and the project is paid for through the Net Zero Energy Revenue Bond after a September 9 vote by the Burlington Electric Commission.

[Jurors to begin deliberations after closing arguments Monday in attempted murder trial](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/18/jurors-to-begin-deliberations-after-closing-arguments-monday-in-attempted-murder-trial)

❝

*"Awartani has attended the trial daily, seated in a wheelchair along the front row of the courtroom gallery directly behind the prosecutors," per VTDigger.*

Closing arguments are today in the trial of Jason Eaton, 51, and the jury gets the case right after. Both sides rested Friday afternoon and Judge John Pacht sent jurors home rather than start lengthy closings, with court resuming at 8:30 this morning. Eaton faces 3 counts of attempted second degree murder in the November 25, 2023 shooting of Hisham Awartani, Kinnan Abdalhamid and Tahseen Aliahmad on North Prospect Street, and the five day trial turned on dueling psychiatric experts and his insanity defense. A conviction on even one count carries up to life in prison without parole.

[When your boss is your landlord: UVM Health Network job cuts put employee housing at risk](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/18/when-your-boss-is-your-landlord-uvm-health-network-job-cuts-put-employee-housing-risk)

❝

*"I just don’t know where to go. I have to find a home for my family and I. And there’s really nothing out there," per WCAX.*

Seven of the employees losing their jobs in the UVM Health Network cuts also live in housing the network owns, part of a $9 million investment in two complexes with nearly 200 units built to attract workers. They get at least 105 days or the end of their lease, whichever is longer, before they have to move out. Sandy Greene, a licensed nursing assistant on Baird 3 for five years, pays $2,600 a month and learned last Tuesday that her unit is closing. Rep. Ashley Bartley of Fairfax says Vermont law barely regulates employers who are also landlords, and a United Way coordinator is working with all seven tenants.

[Burlington Is Poised to Elect Newbies to the Vermont House](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/election-2026/burlington-is-poised-to-elect-newbies-to-the-vermont-house)

❝

*"Without seniority, legislators don’t have as much success in pushing legislation through and getting what they want done," per Seven Days.*

Burlington will likely send at least six new faces to Montpelier in January out of its 11 House seats, right as the city loses its most powerful voices there. House Speaker Jill Krowinski and Senate President Pro Tem Phil Baruth are both leaving, along with Rep. Kate Logan, who chaired the Progressive caucus, and Rep. Tiffany Bluemle, the ranking member on House Appropriations. The incoming class includes 28 year old Jeffrey Peterson, a Democratic Socialists of America member who won his primary without ever holding office, and two Democrats who won their primaries as write ins after nobody filed. Former city councilors Karen Paul, Ali Dieng and Dave Hartnett are also on the ballot, and Brian Cina and Carol Ode, both elected in 2016, become the longest serving Burlington reps.

[Vermont funded lawyers for tenants facing eviction. The program is out of money.](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/18/vermont-funded-lawyers-for-tenants-facing-eviction-the-program-is-out-of-money)

❝

*"You’re going in for surgery, and the surgeon hands you the scalpel and says, ‘I’ll talk you through it,’" per VTDigger.*

Vermont Legal Aid's Tenant Representation Pilot Project runs out of state money on October 31, after lawmakers funded it with just over $1 million in the 2024 session. That is how project director Sara Kagle describes facing eviction court without a lawyer. The pilot put attorneys beside tenants in Lamoille, Windsor, Rutland and Windham counties with no strict income limits, and of the 207 cases resolved through August, attorneys prevented eviction in 73. One was Steve Koren of Brattleboro, whose landlord filed to evict him over $200 in claimed back rent before Legal Aid got the case dismissed in July. Rep. Marc Mihaly, who chairs House General and Housing, said his committee did not push to keep the funding going and struggled to recall exactly why.

[AOE Instructs School Boards to Hire Superintendents for One Year Only](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/education-news/aoe-instructs-school-boards-to-hire-superintendents-for-one-year-only)

❝

*"She’s acting as if the transformation process is a given, and nothing about this transformation is a given," per Seven Days.*

Education Secretary Zoie Saunders is telling districts that are searching for a superintendent to hire a one year interim only, arguing it avoids costly buyouts if districts merge. Critics point out that Act 170 calls for voluntary mergers and gives districts until August 2029, and that state law gives the secretary an advisory role in hiring without requiring her consent. Mountain Views Supervisory Union plans to advertise a two year job anyway, while Addison Northwest asked for a two year contract and was denied. About 20 merger committees have to hold first meetings by October 15, and 11 school boards have already asked to switch groups, including Harwood, which voted to join a committee with Champlain Valley and Mount Mansfield.

[School enrollment on steady decline](https://www.willistonobserver.com/community/schools/school-enrollment-on-steady-decline/article_548f66c9-60a3-492c-b1bc-f80f9b9c08ff.html)

❝

*"Like most of Vermont, we are declining," per the Williston Observer.*

The Champlain Valley School District is still the biggest in Vermont at 3,601 students, but it has lost nearly 300 of them, 7.4%, since fall 2022. The annual demographic study also found that about 25% of students now come from economically disadvantaged families, up from about 15% four years ago, and 20 students are experiencing homelessness, nearly double the past two years. The graduation rate is 95%, well above the 82% state average.

[US-Canada trade war further taxes small businesses as costs rise during the Iran war](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/16/us-canada-trade-war-further-taxes-small-businesses-costs-rise-during-iran-war)

❝

*"The backlash on the market side is actually what’s affecting us the most," per the Associated Press via WCAX.*

Jasper Hill Farm in Greensboro sits about 40 miles from the border, and co-founder Mateo Kehler says Canadian wholesale customers have canceled holiday orders even though cheese was not among the newly tariffed products. He blames a consumer boycott stirred up by the political rhetoric. It is one slice of a wider Associated Press look at small businesses caught in the latest round, in which Canada put reciprocal tariffs on about $20 billion of U.S. goods and the U.S. said it would ban Canadian wine, whiskey, some motorcycles and whey. Analysts expect a muted effect overall since the tariffs cover about 5.5% of trade between the two countries, but some small cross border businesses face taxes of up to 50%.

[Finding healing through dragon boat racing](https://www.charlottenewsvt.org/2026/09/17/finding-healing-through-dragon-boat-racing)

❝

*"For us, the sunflower has become more than a flower. It represents strength, resilience, friendship and the light we find in one another," per The Charlotte News.*

Lori York, who runs Charlotte's newly renamed Adult Community Center, is new to dragon boat racing and just got back from the International Breast Cancer Paddlers Commission festival in Aix-les-Bains, France, the largest ever, with more than 4,500 survivors on 156 teams from 25 countries. Her crew, the Sunflower Sisters, is named for the flowers fellow survivor Eugenie Doyse of Last Resort Farm in Monkton donates every week.
