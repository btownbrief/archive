## Weather & Monday Update:

Happy Labor Day! It’s a sunny start to finish with a high in the upper 70s, dew points in the low 50s and no rain at any hour, which covers every picnic and block party in full. Tuesday may start with a leftover shower that wraps up by mid morning, then settles into a mix of sun and clouds in the low to mid 70s. Wednesday is the one to plan around, cloudy all morning, then a 70 percent chance of showers arriving in the afternoon with a south wind gusting to 30, and showers and thunderstorms after 8 PM. Thursday flips it, with a few morning showers giving way to a drier afternoon in the low to mid 70s, before Friday turns sunny and noticeably cooler with highs in the 60s. Track it all week, with patio, swim, and sunset scores, at [Burlington Right Now](https://guide.btownbrief.com/weather.html).

## Local News (All Links Clickable)

**[38 new affordable apartments open in downtown Burlington](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/06/38-new-affordable-apartments-open-in-downtown-burlington)**

❝

*"Today we get to celebrate this result, a place for our community, for our veterans, for people who are experiencing homelessness, a place where our neighbors can live with dignity, safety, and affordability," per VTDigger.*

The Post Apartments, a 5 story building from Champlain Housing Trust and Evernorth on the old veterans' hall and shelter site near Main Street and South Winooski Avenue, cut the ribbon Friday with all 38 units already full and subsidized in perpetuity. Rents start at $1,160, 9 units are set aside for people leaving homelessness and 10 carry Section 8 vouchers, and the ground floor holds a new veterans' center plus offices for the Chittenden County Community Justice Center. The $24 million project stitched together 20 funding lines, including $1 million Sen. Sanders earmarked and about $1.6 million in state Brownfields money to clean up the soil next to a gas station. The building is fully electric with rooftop solar.

**[Burlington Scavenger Hunt Sparks 'Bursts of Delight'](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/802much/burlington-scavenger-hunt-sparks-bursts-of-delight)**

❝

*"Whenever we find one of these little clusters of stickers, it's like disproportional happiness," per Seven Days.*

The Peace & Justice Center has hidden 33 clusters of tiny metallic blue peace sign stickers across 24 Burlington parks, with the clues written as poems at btvderive.pjcvt.org. Operations director Kason Hudman relaunched the hunt in July after a tougher pilot last summer, and so far exactly one person has found all 33. One couple discovered Potvin Park along the way, a park they had never heard of.

**[Ruling sets up potential battle of experts in trial of man accused of shooting Palestinian students in Burlington](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/01/ruling-sets-up-potential-battle-of-experts-in-trial-of-man-accused-of-shooting-palestinian-students-in-burlington)**

❝

*"At best, Defendant's motion is another attempt to delay trial," per VTDigger.*

Jury selection starts Thursday in the trial of Jason Eaton, 51, accused of shooting and wounding three Palestinian college students in Burlington over Thanksgiving 2023, and Judge John Pacht has cleared the way for a duel of forensic psychiatrists. The defense tried to bar the prosecution's expert, Dr. Jihlam Biswas of Brigham and Women's Hospital, arguing she is not licensed in Vermont, and Pacht rejected that since her role is forensic rather than treating. Eaton has pleaded not guilty to 3 counts of attempted murder and is pursuing an insanity defense, so the two experts' read on his state of mind is the whole case. Hisham Awartani, one of the 3 students, remains paralyzed.

**[Vermont Community Newspaper Group Bought by National Journalism Nonprofit](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/vermont-community-newspaper-group-bought-by-national-journalism-nonprofit)**

❝

*"We don't have quarterly reporting to shareholders, and we don't have analysts looking for a particular kind of growth, and we don't have debt to service when we do an acquisition," per Seven Days.*

The Other Paper, the Shelburne News and the Citizen, along with the Stowe Reporter and the News & Citizen, now belong to the National Trust for Local News, a Colorado nonprofit that runs more than 50 papers and paid a nominal fee for the group. Majority owner Bob Miller and his partners said postal costs and collapsing print ads had all but wiped out profitability, and the trust's CEO pledged no layoffs for the 15 full time staff, 4 of them reporters.

**[Liquor liability insurance still a challenge for some Vermont bars, despite reforms](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/04/liquor-liability-insurance-still-challenge-some-vermont-bars-despite-reforms)**

❝

*"I think it's a fair assessment that it's an uneven terrain that businesses are dealing with for liquor liability. That over 50% threshold -- it's still tenuous; under 50%, a little bit of relief," per WCAX.*

Lawmakers rewrote Vermont's dram shop laws 3 years ago after premiums spiked, and the Vermont Chamber of Commerce says the reforms helped places that make less than half their money on alcohol, while bars and music venues above that line are still struggling to get covered. Location, hours and even the type of music played all feed into the risk math, and state officials say it will probably take a lawsuit against a venue and a court ruling to show whether the changes did any good.

**[Nonprofit Pulls South Burlington Shelter Proposal](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/housingcrisis/nonprofit-pulls-south-burlington-shelter-proposal)**

❝

*"We cannot successfully site and operate a shelter without partnership from local leadership and a common purpose around addressing homelessness," per Seven Days.*

CVOEO has dropped its plan for a 100 bed low barrier shelter at the former mattress store at 1717 Williston Road, cancelling a September 8 city council briefing after South Burlington staff sent councilors a memo arguing the project was doomed. The memo leaned on the pandemic era Holiday Inn shelter, where police logged more than 575 calls and 200 directed patrols over about 14 months, and argued guests would face a 55 minute two bus trip or a 3.5 mile walk to reach services in Burlington's Old North End. Executive director Paul Dragon says he will keep looking for a community willing to partner, which leaves Burlington officials right where they were, frustrated that the Queen City carries a borderless crisis mostly alone.

**[The Vermont Republican Party wants him elected Chittenden County's top prosecutor. He doesn't want the job.](https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/03/the-vermont-republican-party-wants-him-elected-chittenden-countys-top-prosecutor-he-doesnt-want-the-job)**

❝

*"I do not believe we can afford four more years of Sarah George's overly lenient, catch-and-release prosecutorial policies," per VTDigger.*

For the first time, Sarah George has a Republican opponent in November, and he is Jace Laquerre, a Milton software engineer who is not a lawyer and says he will resign the moment he wins so Gov. Scott can appoint someone else to the 4 year term. Bram Kranichfeld won the GOP line on 455 write in votes after losing the Democratic primary to George 49% to 46%, then withdrew, and party chair Paul Dame did not want to leave the ballot line empty. George calls it an open attempt to co-opt a local election, and the governor's office says Scott had no involvement and has never met Laquerre.

**[Bats don't get cancer like humans do. Vermont scientists helped uncover one possible explanation](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-09-03/uvm-scientists-helped-uncover-possible-explanation-bat-longevity-cancer)**

❝

*"Because bats are exceptional in these ways, it tells us something about how extreme longevity can evolve," per Vermont Public.*

UVM researchers are co-authors on a new study in Nature that mapped the genomes of 8 closely related bat species, some living 3 years and some, like Vermont's own little brown bat, 40 or more. The long lived bats carry multiple copies of immune system genes most mammals have only one of, and they are unusually good at finding and eliminating cells with damaged DNA. UVM's Elise Lauterbur compares the little brown bat's lifespan to a human living a thousand years, and the team thinks the findings could eventually point toward gene therapy for cancer.

**[Service Providers Tackle Problems at Burlington's 'Situation Table'](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/topic/burlington/service-providers-tackle-problems-at-burlingtons-situation-table)**

❝

*"I can't express how helpful having them in the city is. I really feel like they're the glue at times," per Seven Days.*

Every week, representatives from more than 30 agencies sit down at Burlington's situation table to hear cases and coordinate help for the people police and firefighters keep running into, and since July 2025 the table has taken on 102 cases and connected nearly 60 people to services. The context is stark, with the police department down from 92 officers in 2020 to 55 and only 44 available for active duty, and just 56 people accounting for 18% of all police incidents in the first half of this year. The Fletcher Free Library's hot spot program, with a security guard and a Howard Center social worker, is the model's clearest win so far. Interim Chief Shawn Burke says the August drug arrests in City Hall Park were about optics.

**[On Hinesburg's Lake Iroquois, loons reflect Vermont's conservation comeback](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/01/hinesburgs-lake-iroquois-loons-reflect-vermonts-conservation-comeback)**

❝

*"It's really rare that you can say that about a species, that significant of a comeback," per WCAX.*

Two loon chicks hatched on a nesting raft at Lake Iroquois this summer, watched over by a volunteer who kayaks out to check on them, and statewide the numbers have climbed from about 15 nesting pairs in the 1990s to 115 last year. Vermont Fish and Wildlife credits the rafts, education and the ban on lead sinkers, and next season volunteers will pull the rafts from a handful of lakes to see whether the loons can now nest on their own.

**[Community College of Vermont Is Thriving Despite Headwinds](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/gen-zero/community-college-of-vermont-is-thriving-despite-headwinds)**

❝

*"We have a structure where, if we don't constantly innovate, we'll be dead," per Seven Days.*

While UVM confirms a projected 7% drop in undergraduates, CCV is on track to grow 9% this fall on top of a decade of steady enrollment around 10,000, making it the state's second largest college on a budget of about $33 million against UVM's $457 million. The recipe in this long Seven Days feature is flexibility and price, with 70 to 75% of classes online, a 2 year degree around $17,000, free tuition through 802 Opportunity for families under $100,000, and more than three quarters of students graduating debt free. Around 100 CCV students transfer to UVM every year, and with 17,000 Vermonters projected to retire annually over the next decade, President Joyce Judy argues the state cannot afford to waste anyone's potential.

## UVM Athletics: Men's Soccer Stays Unbeaten as Field Hockey Splits in Ann Arbor

The men's soccer team has still not lost. No. 11 Vermont [won 2-0 at Sacred Heart on Saturday night](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/9/5/mens-soccer-11-mens-soccer-second-half-performance-lifts-catamounts-past-sacred-heart-2-0.aspx), where Pieter Bultman headed in a Luis Fischer corner in the 50th minute for his third goal in three matches and Luis Vetter curled a free kick over the wall in the 87th for his first goal as a Catamount. Niklas Herceg made four saves for his third clean sheet of the season, and at 4-0-1 the Catamounts are off to their best five match start since 2021. They have scored first in every match this season and have not trailed at any point. Boston University hosts them Tuesday night.

Field hockey went to Ann Arbor for the weekend and came back with a split. Saturday brought a [4-1 win over Kent State](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/9/5/field-hockey-cruises-past-kent-state-4-1-for-first-3-0-start-since-2007.aspx) and the program's first 3-0 start since 2007, with Isi McMahon collecting a career high three points on a goal and an assist, and first career Vermont goals for Emily Enden and Jaime Lewis. Eighth ranked Michigan then [took Sunday's game 4-1](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/9/6/field-hockey-falls-to-no-8-michigan.aspx) behind a hat trick from Esmée de Willigen, though Alicia Battistelli opened the scoring in the first quarter off a feed from Consu de Castro and Ila Gunner made six saves in the cage. The Catamounts are home at Moulton Winder Field on Friday afternoon against LIU.

Women's soccer got its first win of the season, and it took until the last second of the match. [Ella Hall scored off an Éléonore Blouin cross just before the final whistle at Saint Joseph's on Sunday](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/9/6/womens-soccer-last-second-heroics-lift-vermont-past-saint-josephs-on-the-road.aspx) for a 2-1 win and the first goal of her college career, after Eleni Stowell had headed in an Emma Warren cross in the 12th minute. Thursday night at Virtue Field went the other way, with [Boston College winning 5-1](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/9/3/womens-soccer-drop-non-conference-test-against-boston-college.aspx) and Stowell's bending free kick in the 26th minute accounting for the only Vermont goal. Out in St. Albans, [both cross country teams beat UMass Lowell to open the season](https://uvmathletics.com/news/2026/9/5/mens-cross-country-vermont-cross-country-teams-earn-season-opening-wins-over-umass-lowell.aspx), Hinesburg first year Zoey McNabb winning the women's 5k in 17:35.50 and graduate transfer Eamon Burke taking the men's four mile in 20:23.60. The Vermont women swept the top four places and took nine of the top ten.
